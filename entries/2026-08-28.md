Dear diary,

It's officially the end of my first full week of classes here at Caltech, and honestly, I feel like I've just run a marathon while simultaneously trying to solve a Rubik's Cube blindfolded. My brain is buzzing with differential equations, the implications of Schrödinger's cat, and the optimal strategy for the next dorm game night.

The biggest revelation this week? The sheer, unadulterated *freedom*. No parents asking if I finished my homework, no set bedtime, no one telling me to put down the video game. It's exhilarating, but also... terrifying. I mean, my first Math 1a p-set nearly gave me an existential crisis, and that was *before* I got sucked into a three-hour debate about theoretical physics with Alex and Maya in the lounge last night.

And that's where the dreaded word, "self-control," comes in. I woke up this morning feeling like I'd pulled an all-nighter, even though I technically went to bed around 1 AM. It wasn't the Math p-set keeping me up, though it certainly haunted my dreams; it was the sheer magnetism of new friends and new ideas. Every fiber of my being wanted to keep talking, keep exploring, keep *not* thinking about the looming mountain of academic work.

I saw upperclassmen in the dining hall looking like zombies, talking about their "p-set schedule" and "sleep debt," and I totally get it now. It's a delicate balance. How do you fully immerse yourself in this incredible place – the brilliant minds, the fascinating discussions, the absurd dorm traditions – without letting your academic obligations crash and burn?

I’m already seeing my good intentions from Monday morning crumble. I told myself I'd go to the gym three times a week. Didn't happen. I planned to get a head start on that Chem reading. Got distracted by a spontaneous ultimate frisbee game. And the late-night social scene? It’s a siren song. This isn't high school where you can coast a bit. Every single minute feels like it counts here, and there are so many competing demands for those minutes.

So, this weekend's mission isn't just to catch up on sleep (though that's definitely on the list). It's to figure out a realistic strategy for self-control. How do I say "no" to an engaging conversation when I know I *need* to be solving problems? How do I resist the urge to procrastinate until the last possible minute? It feels like the ultimate freshman challenge – mastering myself before Caltech masters me. Wish me luck. I have a feeling I'm going to need a lot of it.

-Trevor
