Dear diary,

August 4th, 2026

Wow. It’s August 4th, and I’m actually here. Trevor at Caltech. Still feels a bit surreal, like I’m in one of those pre-acceptance dream sequences that suddenly shifts to a frantic study session. Move-in day for Lloyd House was… chaotic, but good. Met my roommate, Mark, who seems super chill and is already setting up a ridiculously complex network for his gaming rig. I’m just trying to figure out where all my textbooks will fit, let alone my meager collection of sci-fi novels.

The Pasadena heat is no joke, though. I swear it feels like the air itself is pushing down on you. It got me thinking, actually. I was looking out my window earlier, trying to identify some of the local flora (failed, obviously, but good effort), and my mind just drifted to atmospheric pressure. I’ve always wanted to build a really precise DIY *barometer* – not just a basic water one, but something with a good digital output that I could log data from. Imagine tracking micro-fluctuations linked to localized weather patterns. Maybe a small IoT project for my room once I'm settled? Or a summer UROP next year?

But then it hit me. Caltech itself feels like a giant *barometer*. Every conversation, every intense look from an upperclassman, every syllabus I've glimpsed online – it all feels like a reading of the pressure I'm about to experience. Is the 'pressure' going to be overwhelming, crushing even? Or will it be a steady, challenging force that helps me condense my knowledge and hone my skills, like forming a diamond? I've heard the stories, of course, about the workload and the "techer" intensity.

I guess I'm going to be my own human barometer for the next four years. I'll need to learn how to read my own internal 'pressure gauge,' understand when I'm reaching my limit, and when I can push a little harder. Hopefully, I won't just explode under the strain. (Okay, maybe a slight over-dramatization, but you get the idea.)

It’s exciting, though. And terrifying. Can't wait for orientation to properly kick off tomorrow, and then classes. Time to recalibrate.

-Trevor
