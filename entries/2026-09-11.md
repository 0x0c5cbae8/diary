Dear diary,

Another week down, and my brain feels like it’s been put through a particle accelerator – specifically, the one in my Physics 1a p-set this week. Honestly, I think I spend more time staring blankly at equations than I do actually solving them. Professor Albright moves at warp speed, and the sheer volume of information is just… staggering. But hey, it’s Caltech, right? What did I expect, a leisurely stroll through the cosmos?

I was just trying to clear out my inbox, hoping for an email about the campus rec center schedule or maybe some good news about the upcoming House events. Instead, I got a notification from the student portal: "New Activity on Your Account." My heart did that familiar little freshman flutter of dread. Is it a library fine I don't know about? Did I accidentally break something in the dining hall?

Turns out, it’s a new charge. "IEP Lab Consumables: Wave Optics Module – $45.75." Wait, what? The Introductory Experimental Physics lab – the one where we just spent three hours trying to align a laser and measure diffraction patterns – apparently has separate billing for "consumables." I genuinely thought all that stuff was just… part of the tuition. Or included in the general lab fees. This isn't even for a broken piece of equipment, just for the little bits and bobs we used up.

It's not a huge amount of money, but it's the principle. And the *surprise*. It feels like every corner I turn, there's another hidden cost. First the laundry card, then the "optional" study guide for Calc 1, and now this. I mean, how are you supposed to *experiment* in experimental physics without *consuming* things? What's next, a billing charge for the air I breathe in the lab? (Okay, maybe a bit dramatic, but you get the idea.)

I guess I should probably ask my RA or one of the sophomores in my House about it tomorrow. Or maybe there's some kind of student account office I can visit. It just adds another layer of administrative complexity to an already mind-bending academic schedule. I’m trying to unravel the mysteries of the universe, and instead, I'm trying to unravel the mysteries of Caltech's billing system. It feels like a problem set in itself, but without a clear solution or a TA to ask for help.

Anyway, off to try and get a few hours of sleep before diving back into the abyss of organic chemistry readings. Wish me luck with the billing investigation!

-Trevor
