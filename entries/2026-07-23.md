But hey, at least I made some solid progress on my UROP with Professor Albright – finally got the interferometer readings to consistently show *something* rather than just thermal noise. Big win.

The real mental battle today, though, wasn't with quantum optics, but with something far more insidious: presentation slides. We have our bi-weekly progress meeting tomorrow morning, and I spent a ridiculous amount of time today perfecting the aesthetics for my segment. And by aesthetics, I mostly mean the *font*.

I know, I know. You come to Caltech to unravel the universe's secrets, and here I am, a freshman, agonizing over typography. But honestly, the right font can make or break the readability of a complex graph or a dense equation. I swear, trying to explain the subtleties of photon-photon interactions with a clunky, mismatched **font** is a special kind of hell.

I started, as always, with LaTeX. My initial thought was to just stick with the default Computer Modern, but it felt... a bit too stodgy for the cutting-edge stuff we're doing. Plus, I needed something that would really make the subscripts and superscripts pop without looking cramped. I tried TeX Gyre Termes, then Fira Sans, but neither felt quite right. Too much personality, or not enough. The `fontspec` package nearly drove me insane trying to get everything to render perfectly with `XeLaTeX`. There's a subtle art to kerning and leading when you're dealing with Greek letters and multi-level exponents.

After about an hour of trial and error – an hour I probably should have spent double-checking my error bars – I finally settled on Latin Modern Sans for the main body text and slide titles. It's clean, professional, and has just enough warmth without being distracting. But the real game-changer was using Fira Code for all the snippets of Python code I included to process our data. Those beautiful ligatures, turning `->` into a proper arrow and `==` into a single, elegant symbol? Absolute *chef's kiss*. It just makes the code so much more approachable, even to someone who isn't staring at it all day. Professor Albright probably won't comment, but *I* will know. And it’s the little victories, right?

Sometimes I wonder if my brain is just wired to over-analyze everything, even the smallest details. But then I remember that attention to detail is pretty much the Caltech mantra. Anyway, slides are ready. Data is checked (mostly). Just need to rehearse my spiel tonight. Maybe I'll grab some ice cream from the SAC to celebrate my font victory.
