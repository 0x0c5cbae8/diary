Dear diary,

Another Saturday. The concept of 'weekend' here seems less about rest and more about an uninterrupted block of time to wrestle with problem sets. Today, my wrestling partner was the astrophysics P-set for Professor Ayres' class. Specifically, question 4 – calculating the proper *distance* to a distant galaxy given its redshift and a few cosmological parameters. It felt like I was physically trying to bridge that immense *distance* with just a pen and paper. The sheer scale of it, light-years upon light-years, compressed into a few lines of algebraic manipulation. My brain, I swear, feels like it's stretching and compressing right along with the spacetime fabric I'm attempting to model.

I spent a good three hours staring at the equations, convinced I was off by an order of magnitude, or perhaps even a fundamental constant. The intellectual *distance* between my initial intuition and the correct derivation felt insurmountable for a while. Then, after a particularly strong cup of coffee from The C, it clicked. Not a dramatic 'eureka,' more like a quiet, satisfying snap. The universe, in its vastness, suddenly made a tiny bit more sense, just for a moment. It's a strange kind of intimacy, understanding something so profoundly far away.

Later, while grabbing dinner at Chandler, I found myself thinking about a different kind of *distance*. Thanksgiving break is less than a week away. Home isn't *that* far, just a few hours' flight, but lately it's felt like an astronomical separation. Mom called this morning, asking about my flight, and suddenly the physical *distance* from my family and my old life felt acute. Here, surrounded by other sleep-deprived geniuses, engrossed in the cosmic ballet of differential equations, it's easy to forget about the mundane world outside this intellectual bubble. But the thought of Thanksgiving dinner, the familiar smells, the comfortable quiet of my own bed – it pulls at something deep.

It's a weird dichotomy, isn't it? Spending my days calculating the expansion of the universe, grappling with *distances* that defy imagination, only to be momentarily floored by the relative proximity of home. Perhaps the greatest distance I'm grappling with is the one between who I was a few months ago and who I'm becoming here. Every problem solved, every late night in the library, every new concept assimilated – it all adds to that journey. And the destination? Still a galaxy far, far away, I suppose.

Back to the Lagrangian for the next P-set. This one involves oscillatory motion. More distances to measure, more paths to trace.

-Trevor
