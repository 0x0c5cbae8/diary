Dear diary,

Wednesday again. My internal clock barely registers days anymore, just the relentless march of problem sets. Physics 1c is absolutely *demolishing* my will to live – that last p-set on rotational dynamics had me staring blankly at my whiteboard for three hours straight, convinced I’d fundamentally misunderstood angular momentum or gravity itself. And Ma 1c isn’t exactly a walk in the park either. I swear my brain is just a finely tuned machine for converting caffeine into differential equations.

Tonight, after another... *uninspired*... dinner at Chandler, I hit a wall. My taste buds, much like my sanity, were crying out for something real, something that hadn’t been prepared in industrial quantities. I remembered Mom’s cornbread. The kind she makes with real buttermilk, that’s just sweet enough, with that perfect crumbly-yet-moist texture. A fleeting vision of home amidst the LaTeX and Python.

Impulsively, I decided to try and recreate *something*. I ventured down to the Lloyd House communal kitchen, a place usually reserved for late-night ramen explosions or experimental fermentation projects. Miraculously, it was relatively clean. I rummaged through the small selection of communal pantry items – mostly abandoned spices and half-empty bags of pasta – before heading to Vons. I bought a small bag of **cornmeal**, some milk, an egg, and a tiny container of baking powder. Minimalist cooking for maximum stress relief.

Following a recipe I found on my phone – a "beginner's guide to easy skillet cornbread" – I set to work. It felt… surprisingly therapeutic. Measuring the dry ingredients, whisking the wet. I almost added salt instead of sugar (a common freshman error, I've heard), but caught myself. The batter came together, slightly lumpy but smelling faintly of promise. I poured it into a small, well-seasoned cast iron pan I'd seen lurking in the back of the cupboard – probably belonging to some senior who'd long since graduated.

Twenty minutes in the oven, and the aroma filled the kitchen. It wasn't Mom's. It was… dense. And maybe a *tad* dry around the edges. But it was golden brown, it smelled amazing, and crucially, it was *mine*. I cut a small wedge, slathered it with a tiny pat of butter I'd scrounged, and took a bite. It was gritty, a little less sweet than I wanted, but warm and comforting. A solid 6.5/10 on the cornbread scale, but a 10/10 on the "Trevor-didn't-set-off-the-fire-alarm-and-made-something-edible" scale.

It was a small victory, a tiny anchor in the swirling chaos of problem sets and lectures. Sometimes, you just need a reminder that you can create something tangible, something that isn't just lines of code or derivations. Even if it's just a humble piece of cornmeal bread.

Back to physics now. The cornbread energy boost won’t last forever.

-Trevor
