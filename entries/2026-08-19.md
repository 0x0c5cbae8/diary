Dear diary,

August 19, 2026. Wednesday. Can’t believe it’s actually happening. I’m *here*. Well, technically *almost* here, in my actual dorm room. They let a few of us move in early for the research readiness program, and I’m already feeling that glorious Caltech hum – a mix of intense quiet and the faint whirring of some distant server farm, probably. My room in Avery is… exactly what I expected, and yet not at all. It’s functional, a little sparse, and smells faintly of lemon cleaner and the dreams of a thousand past engineers.

But the most striking discovery wasn't the suspiciously well-organized cable management system in the desk, nor the baffling array of stickers on the closet door (a periodic table of elements where 'Fl' was replaced with 'Frodo'). No, the true gem was nestled on the top shelf of the built-in bookcase: a human **skull**. Not a real one, thankfully, but a remarkably detailed anatomical model. It looked like it had seen some serious study sessions, perhaps even been a silent confidant during late-night problem sets. Its empty eye sockets seemed to gaze out with an almost knowing expression, a silent witness to countless moments of intellectual struggle and Eureka! moments.

I spent a good fifteen minutes just turning it over in my hands. Tracing the sutures, marveling at the delicate architecture of the nasal cavity. It’s a perfect microcosm of the complexity I’m hoping to dive into here. My brain, the very thing I’ll be *racking* over quantum mechanics and advanced algorithms, is housed in something so intricate and elegant. It felt like a bizarre, silent welcome from Caltech itself, a gentle reminder that this place is where the deepest mysteries of the universe, and ourselves, are probed.

Part of me wonders if it belonged to some bio-engineering major who forgot it, or if it’s a traditional 'passing of the torch' from a graduating senior to an eager freshman. Either way, it’s staying. I think I’ll name him 'Cranium.' He’ll be my study buddy, my silent, bone-headed mentor. Maybe he’ll absorb some of the knowledge I'm about to pour into this room. Or maybe he’ll just be a cool conversation starter when my actual roommate arrives next week.

The anticipation is palpable. My own skull is already buzzing with the thought of classes starting. Time to unpack the rest of my boxes, and maybe give Cranium a little dusting.

-Trevor
