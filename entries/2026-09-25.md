Dear diary,

Another Friday, another week where my brain feels like it's been put through the Large Hadron Collider and then asked to reassemble a p-set. Ph 1a is officially my nemesis, and Ma 1a isn't far behind. And don't even get me started on the Ch 1a lab report that ate three hours of my life in the common room last night with Sarah, who I swear is already a chemistry genius.

But the actual stressor today wasn't the academic grind, as usual. It was lunch in Chandler with Liam, a senior from Lloyd. He was talking about his SURF project last summer, something about exoplanet atmospheres and how they're analyzing atmospheric escape, and then casually dropped the bomb: "You really should start thinking about who you want as a **supervisor** for next summer, Trevor. The good labs fill up fast."

A *supervisor*. The word just hung there, heavy as a black hole. Liam made it sound so straightforward, like choosing a meal plan. But I barely know how to choose which laundry machine to use in Ruddock without a minor existential crisis, let alone cold-email a world-renowned astrophysicist or quantum mechanic and ask them to *supervise* my work. What work? I'm still trying to figure out why the vector calculus in my Ma 1a p-set decided to spontaneously combust my brain cells.

It's such a different dynamic than high school. My old science teacher, Mr. Harrison, was a mentor, a guide, someone I knew and trusted. Here, the idea of approaching Professor Anya Sharma, whose work on dark matter I vaguely understand from her Wikipedia page, and asking her to be my **supervisor** for a SURF project... it just feels so presumptuous. What do I even say? "Hello, esteemed Professor, I'm a freshman who occasionally gets a B- on a p-set, and I'd love for you to *supervise* my theoretical contributions to the fundamental mysteries of the universe"? It's intimidating to think about the kind of intellect and dedication a **supervisor** here would expect. I mean, my only "research" experience is a science fair project on potato batteries.

It just feels like another one of those "Caltech challenges" nobody really warns you about until you're neck-deep in p-sets and suddenly realize you're supposed to be planning your research career simultaneously. Maybe I should just focus on not getting eaten alive by Core classes first. One terrifying, supervisor-less step at a time.

-Trevor
