Dear diary,

It’s Sunday, August 9th, and I’m officially a Caltech student. Well, almost. Frosh Camp has been… an experience. My brain feels like it’s running on fumes and pure caffeine, and I’m pretty sure I've met enough brilliant people in the last three days to populate a small country of Nobel laureates. Every single person here is just *so* interesting, and the conversations go from quantum mechanics to the best ramen in Pasadena in like, three seconds flat. It's exhilarating, but also utterly exhausting.

The problem, if you can even call it a problem when you’re living the dream, is sleep. Or the distinct lack thereof. We’ve been up late every night, either bonding over late-night snacks in the lounge, or spontaneously debugging someone’s robotics project, or just trying to process everything that happened that day. My body clock, which was already a bit wonky from the flight, has officially given up and is now operating on some kind of chaotic, pseudo-random schedule.

I’ve been thinking a lot about melatonin. Seriously, *a lot*. My roommate, Mark, mentioned he popped one last night and passed out cold, waking up feeling "surprisingly functional." Meanwhile, I was staring at the ceiling, mentally going over the Schrödinger equation and then immediately pivoting to the optimal strategy for the campus-wide scavenger hunt tomorrow. It's like my brain knows it's finally at Caltech and it just refuses to shut down for fear of missing something important, even when that something important is just REM sleep.

I pulled up a few NCBI articles on my phone during a break this afternoon – classic Trevor move, I know. I was looking into the half-life, optimal dosages for sleep phase shifting versus general insomnia, and the potential long-term effects of exogenous supplementation. Don't want to accidentally mess up my endogenous circadian rhythm permanently right as I'm starting college. But then again, is it really "messing it up" if it's already completely trashed? It feels less like a crutch and more like a physiological hack to survive the sheer awesome intensity of this place.

The irony isn't lost on me: I'm at my dream school, surrounded by incredible opportunities, and all I really want right now is for my pineal gland to get its act together. Maybe I’ll try a low dose tonight, just 0.5mg or 1mg, enough to signal to my brain that it’s time to power down for a bit. I need to be sharp for the rest of Frosh Camp, and definitely for when classes officially start. Can’t derive Maxwell’s equations on three hours of fractured sleep.

Wish me luck, both with the sleep and with navigating this brilliant, beautiful chaos.

-Trevor
