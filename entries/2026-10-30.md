Dear diary,

October 30, 2026. Friday.

Another Friday night, another PSET graveyard. My desk is a warzone of open textbooks, half-eaten granola bars, and crumpled scratch paper. I’ve been staring at Physics 1a PSET 6, problem 3, for what feels like eons. It’s about the annihilation of a positron and an electron, converting their combined mass into pure energy. "Calculate the wavelength of the resulting gamma rays," it says. Simple enough, in theory. But after six hours of trying to make my brain cells perform the relativistic gymnastics required, I’m starting to question the fundamental *matter* of my own existence.

This whole course makes you think so much about what *constitutes* matter, how it transforms, how energy and mass are just two sides of the same coin. It's mind-bendingly cool, don't get me wrong. But at 11 PM on a Friday, when my eyes are burning and my stomach is rumbling, it also makes me wonder what *truly matters*.

Does it matter if I get this specific PSET question perfectly right, or if I just get the general gist? Caltech makes everything feel like it matters infinitely. Every point, every PSET, every class. It’s relentless. Sometimes I worry I’m losing sight of the other things that *matter*: like getting enough sleep, calling my parents, or, you know, having an actual conversation that isn't about quantum fields or orbital hybridization.

There’s a Halloween party planned for tomorrow in Page House, and I’m half-tempted to go as a blob of dark matter – unseen, mysterious, and probably just as confused as I am. But first, I have to conquer this problem. Maybe if I stop thinking of the electron and positron as particles and just as abstract numbers, something will click.

Or maybe I just need more coffee. Either way, this gamma ray calculation *matters* right now. Back to the grind.

-Trevor
