Dear diary,

It’s August 20th, 2026, and I’m actually *here*. At Caltech. My dorm room is still mostly a collection of boxes, but I’ve managed to set up my desk, which feels like a small victory. It’s surreal. I keep looking out the window, half-expecting my old backyard, but instead, it’s other dorm buildings and a glimpse of those iconic palm trees. I got here a bit early for the pre-frosh research orientation, and it's been a whirlwind of meeting a few other early arrivals and trying to figure out where everything is. My brain is already buzzing, even before classes start.

Tonight, after a rather sparse "welcome" dinner from a slightly-too-enthusiastic grad student, I realized I was still starving. The dining hall hours are weird this early. I remembered seeing a common kitchen down the hall. Operation: Midnight Ramen was a go.

I grabbed a pack of instant noodles and then, in a stroke of what I thought was culinary genius, remembered my mom always said a little *butter* makes ramen richer. "Adds a lovely depth," she'd say. So, I grabbed a stick of Land O'Lakes from my mini-fridge.

My plan was simple: boil water, add noodles, then stir in a pat of butter. But then my engineering brain kicked in. What if the butter didn't integrate well? What if it just sat on top? Clearly, the solution was to melt it *first*. So, I put a small saucepan on the stove, tossed in a generous chunk of butter, and turned the burner to medium-high.

Mistake number one. The butter, rather than slowly melting into a golden pool, decided to brown aggressively in about ten seconds flat. Then, it started to smoke. A lot. The fire alarm, thankfully, didn't go off, but the pungent, burnt-dairy smell certainly permeated the tiny kitchen. I panicked, snatched the pan off the heat, and stared at the dark, foamy mess, which now looked less like "lovely depth" and more like "carcinogenic sludge."

I ended up just boiling the ramen with the seasoning packet, sans butter. It was fine. Edible. But the whole experience got me thinking. Here I am, a Caltech freshman, about to tackle advanced physics and calculus, and I nearly set off the smoke detectors trying to make instant ramen with a stick of *butter*. It's a stark reminder that while I might understand the theoretical thermodynamics of phase changes and heat transfer, the practical application in a kitchen is a whole other P-set.

It’s exciting, though. Every small blunder, every new experience, feels like another data point in the grand experiment that is Caltech. I have a feeling I'm going to learn a lot more than just equations here. Even if some of those lessons come with a slight smell of burnt butter.

-Trevor
