Dear diary,

It’s officially Friday night, or rather, early Saturday morning if I’m being honest. The clock on my desktop says 1:17 AM. Just submitted my Ma 1c P-set – barely. My brain feels like it’s been run through a centrifuge, then left to bake in the Pasadena sun. I swear, the calculus problems multiply faster than rabbits; it's like every time I solve one, two more pop up, demanding my attention with their smug little delta-epsilons.

Today was a blur of lectures and office hours. Bi 1, then Ph 1, then a frantic dash to grab some food before diving into the Ma 1c dungeon. The thing about Caltech is, you get so deep into the weeds, sometimes your mind just… wanders. And tonight, for some reason, it wandered to baobabs.

I was in the library, trying to unravel some particularly gnarly proof, when my eyes drifted over to a copy of *The Little Prince* tucked away on a shelf. And just like that, BAM, baobabs. I remember reading it ages ago, but it hit differently tonight. The way the Little Prince had to constantly, diligently, pull out the baobab shoots before they grew too big and cracked his tiny planet apart.

It felt... painfully familiar. Like those baobab sprouts are all the assignments and lectures and research proposals and club meetings that constantly threaten to overwhelm my small freshman planet. If you don't tackle them immediately, if you let even one root take hold, it’s game over. Or at least, a lot of late nights and cold coffee. It's not just the sheer volume, it's the insidious way they can grow unnoticed until suddenly, you're looking at a tree with a trunk thicker than your entire dorm room, demanding an essay due yesterday.

I mean, objectively, baobabs are incredible trees. Giant, ancient, store water, iconic. But right now, the image of them just makes me think of the relentless vigilance required to not let them take over. Is that what Caltech is? A constant weeding of intellectual baobabs, making sure your planet doesn't get consumed? Maybe.

Anyway, the Ma 1c monster is slain for now. I should probably get some sleep before the next batch of baobab sprouts appear. Or before I start seeing actual baobabs in my dreams.

-Trevor
