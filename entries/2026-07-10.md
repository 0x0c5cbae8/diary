Dear diary,

Another Friday. You'd think summer would be a bit less intense, but SURF definitely has its own brand of pressure. My Drosophila genetics project is actually going really well – finally got those CRISPR-modified lines to express the reporter protein consistently. Dr. Aris is even talking about a preliminary abstract for the GSA conference. That’s insane for a freshman!

But here’s the thing. My eyes have been driving me absolutely *mad* this week. It started as just a little itch, like I hadn't slept enough (which, let's be real, is true most days). But by Wednesday, I was full-blown tearing up every time I looked at a microscope, and my nose feels like it’s decided to become a firehose. I tried to apply a logical, systematic approach, of course. Hypothesized it was the dust in the old lab, or maybe the specific strain of yeast we feed the flies, or even the new batch of ethanol we got. I cross-referenced the MSDS sheets, checked for volatile compounds in the lab air (using my portable sensor – yes, I carry one, don't judge), and even tried to correlate my symptoms with specific lab procedures. I compiled a pretty neat spreadsheet, actually, tracking exposure times versus symptom severity.

The data, unfortunately, is inconclusive. Or rather, it strongly suggests *something* in the lab is triggering it, but not any of the variables I initially isolated. Which is frustrating, because if I can't even diagnose my own physiological response with data, what hope do I have for groundbreaking research?

Dr. Aris noticed me looking like I'd just chopped an entire bag of onions this afternoon. She asked if I was getting enough sleep, then looked at my red-rimmed eyes and said, "Trevor, have you ever considered seeing an allergist? Sometimes lab environments can reveal... unique sensitivities."

An allergist. The thought hadn't even crossed my mind. My initial reaction was to dismiss it – I don't *have* allergies, I'm just tired. But then I started thinking. What if it's not the dust, or the yeast, or the ethanol? What if it's the chitin from the flies themselves? Or some obscure protein in the nutrient agar? That would be a fascinating immunological response to characterize, actually. A potential new research avenue... no, no, stop thinking like that, Trevor. First, deal with the actual problem.

So, it looks like my meticulously compiled symptom log will be getting a new recipient. I spent the last hour before dinner Googling "Pasadena allergist reviews" and trying to figure out how Caltech's student health insurance works for off-campus specialists. It feels like admitting defeat, needing professional help for something I should be able to deduce myself. But then again, a true scientist knows when to consult an expert. I just hope they can figure out what's turning my face into a perpetually leaky faucet before I accidentally contaminate my fly stocks with snot.

Wish me luck. And maybe some Kleenex.

-Trevor
