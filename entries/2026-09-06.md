Dear diary,

It's Sunday evening, and honestly, the concept of a weekend here at Caltech is already feeling like a myth. Classes officially started this past week, and my brain feels like it's been through a high-speed centrifuge. Physics 1a with Professor Thorne is... intense. It's exhilarating and terrifying all at once. And don't even get me started on the problem sets. I thought I was good at math. Apparently, "good" is relative, and the average Caltech student's "good" is my "barely hanging on."

I was sitting in the dining hall earlier, trying to decipher a particularly nasty differential equation for my Ma 1a homework, and I caught myself just staring at my hands. I started thinking about high school – a mere few months ago, but it feels like a lifetime. That version of me, the one stressing about AP scores and college applications, seems like an entirely different person. He was so focused, so sure of what success looked like. I wonder what my high school counterpart would make of me now, hunched over a plate of mediocre pasta, questioning every life choice that led me to a problem involving quantum tunneling on a Sunday night. He'd probably be impressed by the sheer volume of LaTeX I'm already producing, but utterly bewildered by the constant low hum of existential dread mixed with pure intellectual joy.

The weirdest thing, though, is realizing everyone else here is going through something similar. I met a guy, Alex, from Dabney House, in our intro CS lab today. We were both debugging the same infuriating syntax error for nearly an hour, and we just clicked. He's got this dry sense of humor, and he explained a concept to me that I just wasn't grasping. It felt like I'd found my coding counterpart – someone who understands the specific flavor of frustration that comes with a seg fault, but also the elation when it finally runs. It's comforting, in a strange way, to know there are others on this insane journey, even if they're all brilliant in their own unique ways. Maybe this feeling of being overwhelmed isn't just me being inadequate, but just... Caltech.

Anyway, gotta get back to that differential equation. Professor Thorne waits for no man.

-Trevor
