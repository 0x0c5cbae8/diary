Dear diary,

Tuesday, July 28, 2026

Another day, another stack of papers on my desk. My UROP is really starting to kick into high gear, and honestly, sometimes I feel less like a freshman and more like a grad student drowning in literature reviews. Dr. Chen’s lab is fascinating, though. We’re working on early embryonic development models, trying to understand the cellular mechanisms that lead to successful implantation and differentiation.

Today was particularly heavy. I spent most of the afternoon sifting through studies on chromosomal abnormalities and early developmental arrest. It’s all incredibly complex, a ballet of gene expression and cellular signaling, where one wrong step can cascade into… well, into a lot of things. And one of the terms that kept coming up, over and over, was "miscarriage."

It’s one thing to see it as a data point, a statistical percentage in a cohort study. It's another to sit there, staring at the clinical descriptions, the biological pathways, the sheer fragility of life unfolding at a microscopic level, and really *think* about what that word means. We’re talking about the fundamental failure of a biological process that is, in essence, trying to build a human being. It’s not just a medical term; it’s a profound loss, wrapped up in immense biological complexity.

The papers didn't focus on the emotional aspect, obviously. They were about aneuploidies, syncytiotrophoblast formation, the role of specific cytokines. But even through all the dense scientific jargon, the reality of it hit me. Here I am, a Caltech freshman, trying to understand the minutiae of cell division, and I’m confronted with one of the most common and often heartbreaking biological events. It makes you realize just how delicate and intricate life truly is, and how much we still don't understand.

It’s not often I get pulled out of my purely scientific headspace like that, but today, that word, appearing so frequently in the context of cellular failure, just landed differently. It’s a sobering reminder that all the equations and models we build are ultimately trying to grasp something incredibly precious and often incredibly vulnerable. Definitely gives you a different perspective on why we do all this research in the first place.

Time to hit the dining hall before it closes, and then maybe try to make sense of these qPCR results.

-Trevor
