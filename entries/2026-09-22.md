Dear diary,

Tuesday. Another Tuesday already. My brain feels like it’s been put through a cosmic tumble dryer. Ph 1a was… Ph 1a. Professor Thompson spent an hour on tensors and I swear half the room (me included) just collectively short-circuited. The P-set for Ma 1a is due tomorrow morning, and I'm still staring blankly at problem 5, feeling like an imposter who somehow snuck into a Mensa meeting.

I remember thinking, back in July, how exciting it would be to come to Caltech. A whole new environment, a chance to start fresh. I really craved a *clean slate*. A place where no one knew "high school Trevor," where I could dive headfirst into all the things I'm passionate about without the baggage of old expectations.

And in a way, it is a clean slate. My House, Blacker, is full of brilliant, quirky people, and I'm slowly figuring out where I fit in. There’s a buzz here that’s genuinely inspiring. But then there’s the other kind of "slate"—the one that Caltech keeps relentlessly writing on. It feels less like a blank canvas for me to define myself, and more like someone is constantly sketching dense circuit diagrams and equations on my brain, faster than I can even process them, let alone commit them to memory. My mental slate just gets crammed tighter and tighter with information, every lecture, every problem set, every late-night discussion.

It’s exhausting, but also… exhilarating? Sometimes I feel like I'm drowning, but then I'll grasp a concept, or figure out a particularly tricky P-set problem, and it's like a little jolt of pure triumph. Tonight, though, I think I just need to wipe my slate clean of tensors and derivatives, even if it’s just for a few hours of sleep. Maybe tomorrow, problem 5 will make more sense. Or maybe I’ll just get a fresh batch of confusion.

-Trevor
