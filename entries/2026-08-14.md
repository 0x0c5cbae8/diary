Found the communal kitchen on the ground floor. It's... functional. Two stovetops, a microwave, and a *dishwasher*. Ah, the mighty dishwasher.

The thing was packed solid, a sedimentary layer of unrinsed pasta bowls and coffee mugs from what looked like several geological eras. No one had run it. Or, perhaps, no one *dared* to run it, given the sheer biohazard contained within. I stood there, ramen bowl in hand, contemplating the thermodynamics of food residue adhesion versus the energy expenditure of hand-washing. My conclusion? Hand-washing was the path of least resistance, if not the path of greatest efficiency.

So I ended up scrubbing my ramen bowl in the shared sink, feeling a pang of… well, not exactly homesickness, but definitely 'dishwasher-sickness.' I wonder how many person-hours are collectively lost at Caltech just due to inefficient dish-cleaning protocols. Someone needs to design a fully automated, self-cleaning, AI-integrated communal kitchen system. Or maybe just a sign that says "Run the damn machine, people." My current model suggests optimal activation before particulate matter forms covalent bonds with ceramic surfaces.

Anyway, tomorrow's orientation. More introductions, more acronyms, probably more shared kitchen conundrums. At least I know where the dish soap is. And that my ramen bowl is now demonstrably cleaner than 90% of the items currently residing in the communal *dishwasher*.
