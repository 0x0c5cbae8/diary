Dear diary,

Tuesday. How is it only Tuesday? My internal clock feels like it's been warped by some kind of gravitational anomaly in the middle of a black hole, and that black hole is called "Caltech Core Curriculum." I swear, the only thing keeping me going is the sheer volume of coffee, which I suspect is rapidly evolving into a new primary food group for me.

Today's Bio 1a lecture with Dr. Chen was particularly intense. We spent two hours diving deep into the intricate mechanisms of **evolution** – not just natural selection, but also genetic drift, gene flow, and the subtle dance of mutations. She showed this incredible simulation of a population adapting to a changing environment, and my brain felt like it was doing a similar rapid-fire adaptation just to keep up. It's fascinating, don't get me wrong, to think about how something so complex and intricate as life can arise from such simple, iterative processes. But then she dropped the bomb about the P-set due Friday, which involves modeling a population bottleneck scenario *and* calculating allele frequencies under various selection pressures. My brain cells are definitely going to be undergoing some serious selection themselves trying to solve that one.

It's funny, though, how the concept of **evolution** doesn't just apply to bacteria or finches anymore. I feel like I, myself, am undergoing a kind of rapid personal **evolution** here. My high school study habits? Extinct. My naive belief that I could get eight hours of sleep a night? A forgotten fossil. My ability to panic silently while still appearing calm and collected? That's definitely an advantageous trait that's evolving quickly. I'm learning to break down problems differently, to collaborate (or at least commiserate) with my House-mates on P-sets, and to accept that I won't understand everything immediately. It's a humbling process, like a single-celled organism slowly developing organelles.

Even the way our study group has formed feels like a microcosm of social **evolution**. At first, it was just whoever happened to be in the lounge when the panic about a P-set started. Now, we've got a fairly stable group – a designated note-taker, a conceptualizer, and two (me included) who are pretty good at grinding through the calculations. Each of us brings a slightly different "gene" to the "gene pool" of problem-solving strategies, and together, we seem to have a higher fitness for survival against the monstrous P-sets.

I guess the takeaway is that everything is always changing, always adapting. Even if right now I feel like a slightly stunned early hominid trying to figure out how to use a club, I know I'm **evolving**. Slowly, painfully, but surely. Maybe one day I'll be an apex predator of problem sets, or at least a highly specialized scavenger. For now, it's back to genetic drift. Wish me luck.

-Trevor
