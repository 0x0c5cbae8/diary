Dear diary,

Another Wednesday in the Pasadena inferno. Seriously, I think the sun and the concrete have decided to conspire to keep the temperature permanently set to "oven." My dorm room fan is fighting a valiant, though ultimately losing, battle against the heat. It’s almost comical how much I crave the *opposite* of this weather right now. A blizzard, maybe? No, that would just lead to a different kind of misery.

Today was one of *those* days in the lab. My SURF project is on optimizing a new type of thermal cloak – basically, trying to make an object "invisible" to heat sensors. I've been running simulations all week, and today, after tweaking a dozen parameters, I finally got a clear result. Except it was the exact *opposite* of what I expected. Instead of diverting the heat around the object, my simulation showed it *concentrating* the heat, creating an even hotter spot! I stared at the screen for a good twenty minutes, convinced the universe was playing a cruel joke on me.

It's moments like these that make me realize how much of Caltech life is just a constant re-evaluation of assumptions. You come in with all these preconceived notions about how things work, how problems are solved, how *you* are going to solve them. Then, almost invariably, the universe (or, more likely, my own flawed logic) throws an answer at you that's the complete *opposite* of what you anticipated. It's frustrating, of course. But there's also something oddly satisfying about tracing back your steps, finding the subtle error – a flipped sign in an equation, a misplaced boundary condition, a forgotten permittivity constant.

It makes me think about other things too. Like how my social life here is often the *opposite* of what my high school friends imagine. They picture wild parties; I picture late-night whiteboard sessions fueled by cheap coffee and debates about quantum entanglement. Or how my self-perception has shifted. I used to think I had a handle on things, but here, I often feel like I'm flailing, learning, growing in ways that are the *opposite* of comfortable. It's challenging, definitely, but also exhilarating. Every "opposite" result, every unexpected turn, feels like another data point in the grand experiment of figuring myself out.

Anyway, I’m going to grab some dinner, probably something cold – definitely the *opposite* of the hot food I usually crave. Then, back to the simulations. Tomorrow, I'll figure out why my thermal cloak decided to become a heat magnet. Maybe it's a feature, not a bug? Or maybe I just need to flip a sign somewhere. Again.

-Trevor
