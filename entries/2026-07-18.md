Dear diary,

Ugh, it’s Saturday. What's a Saturday? Apparently, it’s another day to lose myself in the lab, coaxing data out of stubborn materials. Most of the other SURF students are probably out doing… normal Saturday things. Me? I'm wrestling with perovskites and the mysteries of light, specifically their interaction with electric fields. Dr. Chen’s lab is practically my second home this summer.

Today was all about trying to get consistent readings on the Pockels effect in the lead-free ferroelectric thin films we grew last week. The whole point is to characterize how the material's refractive index changes under an applied electric field, which in turn affects the **polarisation** state of light passing through it. Sounds straightforward, right? *Wrong*.

The laser alignment alone is a nightmare. Then there's the high-voltage setup, the cryostat to keep the sample at a precise temperature, and the photodetectors that seem to have a mind of their own. I spent hours tweaking the quarter-wave plate and the analyzer, trying to compensate for intrinsic birefringence and isolate just the electro-optic modulation. The signal-to-noise ratio is terrible. One minute, I think I've got it, seeing a clear rotation of the **polarisation** ellipse, and the next, it's just noise, probably from some stray reflection or an unstable voltage supply. It feels like the universe itself is conspiring to scramble my data.

It's fascinating, though. Thinking about how the very electrons in the material respond to the external field, creating these tiny dipole moments that collectively orient, causing a macroscopic dielectric **polarisation** that then influences the light wave at the atomic level… my brain feels like it’s vibrating at a thousand hertz just trying to grasp it all. Dr. Chen says it's crucial for next-gen optical modulators, and if we can get these lead-free alternatives to perform, it's a huge step for sustainable photonics.

I swear, sometimes I feel like my brain itself is undergoing some kind of **polarisation**. On one side, there's the intense, almost obsessive focus on this single, minuscule data point, trying to wring meaning out of it. On the other, there’s the overwhelming sense of everything else I *could* be doing, or *should* be understanding, or the sheer magnitude of the problem itself. It’s hard to keep everything aligned.

Anyway, I think I finally isolated a ground loop that was messing with the signal. Fingers crossed tomorrow brings less noise and more clarity. And maybe a few more hours of sleep.
