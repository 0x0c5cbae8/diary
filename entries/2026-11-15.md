Dear diary,

Another Sunday vanishes into the academic void. I swear, these weekends are just extended weekdays with a slightly different set of deadlines. Today was a battleground: first, conquering the Ma 1a problem set, which finally succumbed after a four-hour siege involving copious amounts of coffee and a brief existential crisis about Fourier series. Then, I tried to get ahead on the ME 10 lab report, but I got completely stuck on the data analysis for the cantilever beam deflection. It's like the numbers are actively mocking me.

The sun just dipped behind the San Gabriel mountains, painting the sky in a really pretty mix of orange and purple. Usually, that’s my cue to pack it in, but the *evening* doesn't mean "done" here, it just means "shift gears." The lights are coming on across campus, and I can see people still huddled in their dorm windows, probably staring at similar screens, wrestling with their own p-sets. There's a particular hum to a Caltech *evening*, a quiet energy of collective brainpower at work.

I just grabbed some food from Chandler – thankfully, they had decent pasta tonight. My brain feels like scrambled eggs, but I still have to review the quantum mechanics readings for tomorrow's physics lecture. And if I'm being honest, I promised myself I’d call my parents. Maybe I'll do that after I tackle Schrödinger one more time.

It’s almost 8 PM, and the thought of a "relaxing *evening*" is basically a mythical creature for a freshman. Still, there’s a strange satisfaction in knowing you’re pushing your limits. Just wish those limits weren't quite so elastic sometimes. Hopefully, tomorrow *evening* will be slightly less intense. One can dream, right?

-Trevor
