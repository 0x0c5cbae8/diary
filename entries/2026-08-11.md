Dear diary,

It’s Tuesday, August 11th, 2026, and I'm officially a Caltech student. Or, at least, I'm sleeping in a bed in Blacker House, surrounded by cardboard boxes and enough instant ramen to survive a small apocalypse. The air here already smells faintly of old textbooks and fresh ambition.

Today was a whirlwind. Move-in was... efficient, which is a nice word for 'my parents strategically abandoned me with my carefully inventoried bins and a hug that lasted just a bit too long.' Then came the orientation mixer. So many new faces, all incredibly bright, all radiating that unique blend of terrified excitement that I'm pretty sure is universal among Frosh. I think I introduced myself to someone as "Trevor, like the coefficient of friction," which probably didn't land as well as I'd hoped.

I ended up in a slightly less awkward conversation with Sarah, one of the upperclassmen RAs. She’s a materials science major, which immediately piqued my interest, obviously. But what really caught my eye wasn't something she said about amorphous alloys, but something she was wearing: a simple, elegant silver ring, intricately etched with what looked like a stylized DNA helix.

It was more than just pretty; it was *precise*. My mind immediately started running through the manufacturing process. Was it lost-wax casting? Or perhaps a truly remarkable example of micro-machining? The incredible tensile strength required for those delicate loops, the way the light caught the polished silver – it wasn't just *jewellery*, it was a miniature feat of engineering. I found myself mentally calculating the surface area-to-volume ratio of the helix, wondering about the alloy composition to achieve that specific lustre and hardness without compromising structural integrity. I even started to consider the Young's modulus of hypothetical biological polymers versus the actual silver.

Part of me feels ridiculous for analyzing someone's ring with the same intensity I'd apply to a problem set. But another part, the Caltech part I guess, found it incredibly reassuring. It was a physical manifestation of beauty derived from intricate design and fundamental science. It made me realize that even in the social chaos of orientation, the underlying principles I love are everywhere. Sarah probably just sees it as a cool ring, but to me, it was a tiny, wearable reminder that everything, even something meant to be purely aesthetic, has a deeper, fascinating structure.

Maybe Caltech won't be just a grind of equations. Maybe it'll be about finding that hidden beauty and engineering marvel in everything, from the cosmic microwave background to a tiny piece of silver jewellery.

Time to tackle another box. Or maybe just stare at the ceiling and think about crystallography.

-Trevor
