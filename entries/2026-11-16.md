Dear diary,

Another Monday in the books, or more accurately, another Monday *almost* buried under a pile of problem sets and lecture notes. I swear, the quantum mechanics P-set has more questions than there are stars visible from campus (okay, slight exaggeration, but it feels like it). Just finished debugging the code for my CS project – spent two hours chasing a segmentation fault that turned out to be a misplaced semicolon. The indignity!

I needed a break, a proper brain-reset. So, I leaned back in my chair and stared out the window of my single. The late afternoon sun was starting to dip, painting the sky with those insane Pasadena purples and oranges. And there, way up high, were these delicate, feathery streaks.

**Cirrus.** My brain, bless its over-caffeinated core, immediately started cataloging. Ice crystals, exclusively. Formed at altitudes typically above 20,000 feet, where temperatures are well below freezing. They’re so thin, so wispy, composed of millions of hexagonal ice prisms that scatter light in ways that can create halos or sun dogs, though not today. They’re like nature’s high-altitude calligraphy, spelling out atmospheric conditions we barely perceive from down here. Sometimes I think about how their reflectivity or absorptivity can play a role in Earth's radiative balance, especially the thinner, more transparent ones – tiny, almost invisible players in the global climate game.

It’s funny, in the midst of trying to understand wave-particle duality and the nuances of C++ pointers, taking a moment to just *look* at something so naturally elegant and scientifically profound feels… grounding. Or maybe "ungrounding" is better, as it lifts my thoughts high above the immediate academic pressure.

I wish my progress on my electromagnetism project felt as clear and defined as those high-altitude ice formations. Right now, it’s more like a chaotic cumulonimbus – dense, complex, and probably about to rain down a storm of debugging. Still, seeing those cirrus clouds was a small, perfect pause button. A reminder that there’s a whole universe of elegant physics happening constantly, even when my own small universe feels like it’s collapsing.

Okay, back to the grind. Quantum isn't going to solve itself.

-Trevor
