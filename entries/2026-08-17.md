Dear diary,

Another Monday in the books, or, more accurately, another Monday where I feel like my brain just ran a marathon while simultaneously trying to solve a quantum mechanics problem set from the future. Freshmen year is definitely… *something*. Physics 101 already feels like it's trying to rewire my neural pathways, and I'm pretty sure I spent three hours today trying to debug a single line of Python for my intro CS class. Good times.

What really threw me for a loop today wasn't the insane problem set, though. It was during that ridiculously long "Welcome to Caltech Life" orientation session. They were talking about all the student clubs, campus resources, and eventually, the Caltech Y and the student *government*. My mind just sort of latched onto that word: "government."

I mean, I've never really thought much about it beyond what I read in the news, which mostly seems to be about gridlock and yelling. But hearing them talk about *our* student government, allocating funds for club events, deciding on campus policies, mediating disputes between houses… it suddenly hit me how complex even a small, relatively homogenous system of governance is.

I found myself analyzing it like a circuit diagram. What are the inputs? Student needs, club proposals, faculty advice. What are the outputs? Budgets, rules, events. And what's the feedback loop? Elections, student surveys, protests (probably more like strongly worded emails here). It's designed to keep a community of incredibly smart, opinionated, and often sleep-deprived individuals from devolving into chaos. And yet, there were so many caveats, so many "well, sometimes it's slow," or "we're trying to streamline this process."

It made me wonder. If governing a few thousand hyper-intelligent college students is this challenging, what hope is there for entire nations? I'm here to build things, to solve fundamental problems in physics and computation. But what's the point of discovering cold fusion or inventing true AI if the *government*—the system designed to organize society around these advancements—can't handle them? It feels like the human systems are always lagging behind the technological ones.

Maybe that's a problem for a different kind of engineer, a social engineer or something. Or maybe, eventually, my work will intersect with it. For now, I just have to figure out how to govern my own time effectively enough to finish this P-set before 2 AM. Wish me luck.

-Trevor
