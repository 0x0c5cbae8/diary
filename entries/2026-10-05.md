Dear diary,

Another Monday in the books, or rather, another Monday where I feel like a book that's been thrown down a flight of stairs. Physics problem set 3 is officially trying to break me. Spent three hours staring at the same relativistic momentum question, convinced I've forgotten how to do basic algebra, let alone anything remotely connected to Special Relativity. And that's *after* a full day of lectures – Ma 1a, Phys 1a, and CS 1. My brain feels like a sponge that's been wrung out and then put through a spin cycle.

Dinner in Chandler was a welcome distraction, mostly just commiserating with Liam about the sheer volume of work. He's convinced the profs are just seeing how much sleep deprivation we can handle before we start hallucinating. Honestly, I wouldn't be surprised if I started seeing vectors in my oatmeal.

The only real moment of calm today came from something completely unexpected. Walking back to Avery from the labs, my head still swimming with potential fields, I happened to glance into the common room. Maya, from down the hall, has apparently decided to cultivate a mini-jungle on her windowsill. Among the various succulents and what I think might be a struggling basil plant, there was this absolutely vibrant, deep pink *cyclamen*. I don't know much about flowers – my botanical knowledge extends to "it's green" or "it smells nice" – but this one really caught my eye. It looked so delicate and perfectly formed, almost unreal against the backdrop of our scuffed common room furniture and whiteboard covered in half-erased equations. For a solid thirty seconds, I just stood there, completely absorbed by it. No thoughts of Lagrangian mechanics, no mental debugger running through my C++ code. Just... a flower.

It was a small thing, but it kinda hit me. This place is all about pushing limits, solving the impossible, thinking at the edge of human knowledge. But sometimes, you just need to stop and look at a pretty flower. It felt like a tiny, quiet rebellion against the relentless academic grind. I might need to ask Maya where she got it. Maybe a little splash of non-euclidean beauty is exactly what my desk needs right now. Or maybe I just need to finish this problem set and get some sleep. Probably both.

Okay, back to the grind. Wish me luck with this relativistic momentum; I think it's winning.

-Trevor
