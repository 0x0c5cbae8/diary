Dear diary,

November 24th, 2026. Tuesday. It feels like the sky outside is mimicking my brain – gray, heavy, and threatening to burst. Thanksgiving break is practically here, yet my mind is anything but in a festive mood. More like a 'finals are coming, you are not ready' mood.

I spent the last five hours wrestling with the PH 105 problem set due tomorrow. It’s on quantum perturbation theory, and I swear, every time I think I’ve grasped a concept, Professor Ramirez throws another curveball that makes me question my entire understanding. I’m staring at my final derivation for problem 4, and I have absolutely zero *certainty* that it’s correct. I’ve checked my math, re-read the relevant chapter, even tried a slightly different approach, but that nagging doubt just won’t dissipate. It's not just about getting the right answer; it's about the *certainty* of the method, the *certainty* that I haven't missed some subtle boundary condition or misinterpreted a core principle.

It's a feeling that seems to pervade everything here at Caltech, especially as a freshman. You come in feeling pretty smart, pretty *certain* of your abilities. Then you're hit with problems that don't have clear-cut answers, or concepts so abstract they defy easy comprehension. Professor Ramirez often talks about how science isn't about absolute certainty, but about refining our understanding through evidence and probability. Intellectually, I get it. But emotionally? When my GPA hangs in the balance, I’d trade all the philosophical nuances for a single, comforting beam of *certainty*.

It extends beyond the p-sets too. Am I *certain* I want to major in physics? Am I *certain* I'm even cut out for this place? Sometimes, the imposter syndrome gets so loud, it drowns out any other thoughts. The only thing I feel a true *certainty* about right now is that I need more coffee and maybe a brief escape into a dumb YouTube video. Or the *certainty* that my mom will ask me if I'm eating enough over Thanksgiving break. Small certainties, at least.

Maybe learning to navigate this sea of uncertainty is part of the Caltech experience. But man, it’s a tough lesson.
