Dear diary,

2026-07-11, Saturday. Another Saturday spent staring at cells, which honestly, is my ideal Saturday these days. Who knew freshman summer could feel so intense? Most of my high school friends are flipping burgers or chilling at the beach, and here I am, practically living in Professor Chen's bioengineering lab. But I wouldn't trade it for anything.

Today was all about the vascular tissue project. We're trying to engineer functional capillary networks *in vitro* within a hydrogel scaffold – basically, trying to grow tiny, working blood vessels in a dish. It’s incredibly finicky. You need the right growth factors, the perfect stiffness in the gel, and just the right cell density for the endothelial cells to differentiate and start forming lumens. It’s a delicate dance of biochemistry and biomechanics.

I spent probably six hours meticulously seeding cultures and then checking the microscope. For a while, it just looked like a mess of disorganized cells, which is frustrating when you know they *should* be starting to self-organize. It's like they're playing hard to get. I nearly convinced myself I'd messed up the hydrogel preparation again – one slight deviation in the polymerization time and the whole matrix is off, ruining the mechanical cues the cells need to form proper *tissue*. It's almost as fragile as tissue paper sometimes, the entire experiment resting on a hair's breadth of precision.

Then, around 4 PM, I spotted it. A faint but unmistakable hint of lumen formation in one of the dishes. Just a tiny, nascent network, but it was there! My heart almost hammered right out of my chest. It felt like winning the lottery, just for a microscopic bit of *tissue* doing what it's supposed to do. Dr. Lee, the post-doc, just gave a knowing nod and told me to take clearer images next time, but I swear I saw a glimmer of approval in his eyes. He probably gets how much these little victories mean.

It's moments like these that make the late nights and the constant worry about experimental controls worth it. Thinking about how this kind of engineered *tissue* could revolutionize regenerative medicine – growing replacement organs, repairing damaged hearts... it's mind-blowing. And to think I'm even a tiny part of it, as just a freshman SURF student. It’s humbling, terrifying, and exhilarating all at once.

My brain is fried, though. Definitely need to grab some food and maybe *not* dream of endothelial cell migration tonight. And perhaps grab a box of actual *tissue* for my perpetually stuffy lab-induced nose before I head home.

-Trevor
