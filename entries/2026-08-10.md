Dear diary,

Ugh, another Monday. It feels like I've been here a lifetime already, even though O-week doesn't officially start for another couple of weeks. This early arrival for my "pre-SURF" project (unofficial, but Dr. Chen said I could get a head start) is really kicking my butt.

Today was supposed to be the day. The day I finally got the sensor fusion algorithm *implemented* on the tiny ESP32 for the autonomous rover project. I spent all summer designing it, simulating it in MATLAB, tweaking the Kalman filter parameters until they were theoretically perfect for minimizing drift from the IMU and GPS. The mathematical elegance, the theoretical robustness – it was a thing of beauty.

But the *implementation*? That's where the beauty goes to die a slow, painful death, apparently. The real-world data coming off the accelerometer is a noisy mess. The I2C communication with the gyroscope keeps dropping packets sporadically. And don't even get me started on the timing interrupts – trying to synchronize everything while also reading sensor data and sending commands to the motor controllers... it's like trying to conduct an orchestra where half the musicians are playing different instruments at random intervals. My perfectly optimized C++ code from the simulator is just crashing on the hardware, giving me cryptic error codes about memory overflow. It seems that the *implementation* isn't just about translating pseudo-code to C++, but about wrestling with the brutal, unforgiving realities of actual electronics and limited processing power.

It's humbling, to say the least. All those lectures and textbooks make it sound so straightforward: design, code, deploy. They don't quite prepare you for the sheer amount of debugging and low-level tweaking required just to get the system to acknowledge it exists. Professor Sharma in my online intro to EE class kept talking about "the gap between theory and practice," and man, am I living it. I'm going to hit up some of the upperclassmen in the robotics lab tomorrow. Surely, someone’s figured out a robust *implementation* strategy for these cheap IMUs before. Or maybe I just need more coffee.

-Trevor
