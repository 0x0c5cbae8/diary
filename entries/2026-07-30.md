Dear diary,

July 30th, 2026. Thursday.

Another Thursday, another day where my brain feels like it’s been run through a centrifuge set to maximum RPM. SURF is… well, it’s everything they say it is. Intense, exhilarating, occasionally soul-crushing. Today was mostly exhilarating, I think. Got some fantastic preliminary data from the new interferometer setup – the noise reduction algorithm actually seems to be working, which is a minor miracle. Spent six straight hours debugging code, fueled by lukewarm coffee and the vague hope of a breakthrough. The cleanroom air is starting to feel like home.

When I finally stumbled back to my dorm, I realized I hadn't eaten anything substantial since 7 AM. My usual routine is a microwave burrito or instant noodles, but tonight I felt ambitious. I remembered my mom's advice about eating protein, especially when working long hours. "Eggs are your best friend, Trevor," she always says.

So, I decided to make scrambled eggs. Simple, right? Even for a guy who once almost set off the fire alarm trying to boil water. I cracked the first egg, and it was perfect. Into the bowl. The second one… disaster. The shell caught the edge of the pan, and the **yolk** immediately burst, splattering over my hand and the counter. It was this perfectly spherical, vibrant yellow orb one second, then a runny, chaotic mess the next. Just like that, all its structural integrity gone.

For a moment, I just stared at it. It felt oddly profound after a day of trying to maintain precision and order in a complex physical system. All that potential, all that stored energy and nutrients, held within that delicate membrane, just gone in an instant. It made me think about the fragility of even seemingly robust systems – how one tiny, misplaced force can unravel everything. Or, less philosophically, how much I still suck at basic kitchen skills.

Anyway, I cleaned it up and managed to scramble the remaining two eggs (with an unbroken yolk, thankfully). It wasn't gourmet, but it was hot food, and surprisingly satisfying. Maybe the real "yolk" of the Caltech experience isn't just the mind-bending problem sets or the cutting-edge research, but these small, everyday challenges and the lessons you unexpectedly pull from them. Even if it's just learning not to hit the egg on the sharp edge of the pan.

Time to hit the hay. Tomorrow, back to photons and algorithms. Wish me luck.

-Trevor
