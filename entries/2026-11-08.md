Dear diary,

Another Sunday vanishes into the ether, swallowed by problem sets and the faint smell of microwaved ramen from down the hall. Today was particularly grueling, but I ended it with a satisfying, almost *artistic*, moment of clarity.

I spent most of the day holed up trying to finish the PHYS 12c lab report. Specifically, the data analysis for the oscillating magnetic field experiment. You know, the one where we had to map the field strength across different points. Raw numbers from the multimeter are just... numbers. A chaotic jumble of digits that mean absolutely nothing to the untrained eye, and frankly, not much more to the trained one until you process them.

The challenge wasn't just calculating the field, but effectively *displaying* it. I started with a simple scatter plot in Python, but it looked like a toddler had thrown a handful of sprinkles at a black canvas. Utterly uninterpretable. I tweaked the interpolation, adjusted the color mapping, cursed at `matplotlib` for a good hour when it wouldn't *display* the gradient correctly, and then, finally, it clicked.

I debugged a silly indexing error in my numpy array, reran the script, and there it was: a beautiful 3D surface plot, glowing with false colors, clearly *displaying* the undulating peaks and troughs of the magnetic field. The maximum field strength was a fiery red peak, dipping to cool blues where it was weakest, all laid out across the spatial coordinates. It wasn't just data; it was an explanation, a visual argument. The satisfaction of seeing that complex, invisible phenomenon so elegantly *displayed* on my screen was immense. That’s why I’m here, I guess – to take the messy reality and distill it into something understandable, something presentable.

It made me think about how much of Caltech is about *display*. Not just in labs, but everywhere. The constant *display* of ingenuity in hallway whiteboards, the upperclassmen’s research posters *displayed* in the Beckman Auditorium lobby, even the quirky pranks and house stunts that are essentially grand *displays* of engineering and humor. Everyone’s constantly putting their brilliance on *display* here, and sometimes it's intimidating, but sometimes it's incredibly inspiring.

Now, if only I could *display* the same level of elegance in my presentation skills for next week's seminar. Maybe a good night's sleep will help with that.

-Trevor
