Dear diary,

Wow. Just… wow. It’s officially my first Monday living at Caltech, and my brain feels like it’s been defragmented about five times already. Orientation has been a whirlwind of faces, names, and acronyms I’m trying desperately to remember (Frosh Camp? Ditch Day? SURF? Help!). Everyone here is so brilliant and intense, and I keep having imposter syndrome flashbacks, but in a good way? Like, *I'm actually here*.

After a ridiculously long day of scavenger hunts across campus and mandatory 'how not to blow up the chemistry lab' sessions, I finally got back to my room in Lloyd House. My roommate, Kevin, is already face-first in a textbook – I swear he's absorbed half the library in the last two days. I, however, am still attempting the Herculean task of unpacking.

That’s where the *armoire* comes in. This thing is ancient. Seriously, it looks like it belongs in a museum, or maybe Narnia. It’s this colossal dark wood beast, with ornate carvings and handles that feel like they've seen a century of freshmen trying to pry them open. It takes up a solid quarter of my side of the room. My modern, sleek Caltech-issue laptop looks ridiculously out of place next to it. I tried to hang my clothes inside, and the hangers kept getting snagged on something – probably the ghosts of engineering majors past. Kevin just grinned and said, "It's got character, man. Probably heard more secrets than the entire Honor System."

And he’s right, in a weird way. It's a testament to the fact that this place has *history*. Generations of brilliant, sleep-deprived students have lived in this very room, probably cursed this very *armoire* for its sticking drawers, and then gone on to invent quantum-something or solve the universe’s biggest mysteries. It’s a little intimidating, but also incredibly cool. Like I’m stepping into a legacy.

My brain is fried, my clothes are still mostly in a pile on the floor next to the mighty *armoire*, but I’m here. And I’m ready (mostly) for whatever Caltech throws at me next. Even if it involves daily battles with a grumpy wardrobe.

-Trevor
