Dear diary,

It's Tuesday, October 27th, and if I didn't know any better, I'd say I was serving a life sentence. Not in some literal jail, thankfully, but in the metaphorical prison of Caltech's Ph 1a p-set. Seriously, I spent what felt like twenty-four hours straight trying to untangle Problem 6, the one about the particle in an infinite potential well. Every time I thought I'd finally derived the correct eigenfunctions, another layer of mathematical torment would appear, chaining me to my desk in Blacker House, with nothing but a rapidly depleting pot of coffee for company.

I feel like a prisoner of my own ambition, willingly signing up for this ordeal, only to realize the walls are built of differential equations and the bars are made of impossible integrals. My cellmate is imposter syndrome, whispering doubts every time I get stuck. The outside world, with its sunlight and social interactions, feels impossibly far away, like something I can only glimpse through a tiny, grimy window when I briefly step out for more caffeine. Even walking across campus today, I felt this invisible tether pulling me back to my notes, to the unfinished business of quantum mechanics.

There was a fleeting moment this afternoon, in the middle of a particularly dense Ma 1b lecture on Fourier series, where I just wanted to bang on the table and demand an early release. But then, as always, a tiny spark of understanding flickered, and I was re-ensnared, drawn back by the challenge. It’s a strange kind of captivity, isn't it? One where the guards are my own professors, and the warden is my own insatiable curiosity. I guess the only way to earn parole is to solve the damn problem. And then the next one. And the one after that.

Maybe tomorrow, I’ll find a way to break out, even if it's just for an hour or two. Or maybe I’ll just dig my tunnel a little deeper into the textbooks.

-Trevor
