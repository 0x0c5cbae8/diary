Dear diary,

2026-07-09 (Thursday)

It's 1:37 AM. Another Thursday bleeding into Friday. I told myself I'd get to bed before midnight tonight, but then the gravitational pull of the coffee machine in my alley felt stronger than my pillow’s. I’m deep into the pulsar analysis for Dr. Chen’s group this summer, and it’s simultaneously exhilarating and soul-crushing.

Today was mostly soul-crushing. I spent hours debugging this bizarre visual artifact in the `StellarViewPy` library, which we use to render the pulsar emission profiles. It’s a pretty niche open-source tool, developed by a former grad student here, and it usually just *works*. But today, on a particular dataset from the Green Bank Telescope, it started drawing these phantom lines. Like the pulsar was having an existential crisis and trying to redraw its own past.

My first thought was, "My code is garbage." My second thought was, "No, it's gotta be `StellarViewPy`." I dove into the source code on GitHub. Oh. My. God. The last commit was almost two years ago. There are like twenty open pull requests, some from 2024. And the issues tab? A graveyard. A *digital* graveyard of unanswered pleas and bug reports.

That’s when it hit me: the project is effectively unmaintained. The original author, I remember hearing, got hired by SpaceX and probably hasn’t looked back. And I totally get it – who wants to spend their evenings fixing obscure bugs in a library they wrote years ago, when they could be designing rocket engines? But still, it’s a problem. A big, pulsar-shaped problem for *my* research.

It made me think a lot about the role of a **maintainer**. It's such a thankless job, isn't it? You write something cool, you put it out there, and then suddenly you're responsible for its health and well-being long after you’ve moved on mentally. Reviewing PRs, responding to bug reports, wrestling with documentation – it's like digital gardening, but with way more unsolicited weeds and very few flowers of appreciation.

I even tried pinging the original maintainer on an old Caltech email address I found, just on a whim. Probably won't get a reply. So now I'm faced with a choice: either fork the project and become its de facto maintainer for my specific bug, or try to implement a workaround that will probably be even jankier. The thought of adding "StellarViewPy maintainer" to my summer responsibilities next to "freshman" and "SURF researcher" makes my head spin. I barely manage to maintain my own sleep schedule, let alone a whole codebase.

It’s a good lesson though. When I finally write my own groundbreaking code (hopefully!), I need to remember that code isn't just written; it's *maintained*. And if I want it to be useful to others, or even to Future Trevor, I'll need to be a responsible maintainer, or find someone who can be. Otherwise, it just becomes another digital fossil.

Time to brew another pot. Maybe a quick walk around the alley will help clear my head before I try to patch this thing myself. Wish me luck.

-Trevor
