Dear diary,

Another Monday in the books. Or rather, another Monday where I barely feel like I *made* it to the books. This weekend was… a lot. Between the CS 1 problem set that nearly broke my brain, the Physics lab that involved more sparks than I’m comfortable with, and then helping out with the Fleming House *Wreck-It Ralph* themed hallway decorations… I’m running on fumes.

Speaking of sparks and messes, that’s where today’s *highlight* comes in. After the decoration chaos Sunday night, there was glitter *everywhere*. And paint splatters. And general creative debris. Being the eager freshman, I volunteered to help clean up the common room. I grabbed what I thought was an old t-shirt from the "discard pile" by the craft supplies and started scrubbing at a particularly stubborn blob of purple paint. I was really going at it, getting the paint off the floor, feeling like a responsible house member.

That’s when Kevin walked in. He took one look at me, then at the "rag" in my hand, and just started laughing. Not a mean laugh, but one of those deep, wheezy Caltech laughs that makes you wonder if they'll ever breathe again. Turns out, my "rag" wasn't just *any* old t-shirt. It was a vintage Fleming House 'Ditch Day' shirt from the 90s, signed by a bunch of famous alumni who apparently *lived* in this very house. It wasn't in the "discard pile" for cleaning; it was in a bag waiting to be framed for some house history display.

Needless to say, the rest of the day was spent with everyone – Kevin, Sarah, even some of the sophomores – absolutely *ragging* on me for my freshman blunder. "Oh, Trevor, just trying to 'clean up' house history, eh?" "Maybe you can just put *your* name on it now, Trev, since it's practically yours after that scrubbing!" I mean, it’s mostly just good-natured teasing, but still. I swear I saw Professor Smith give me a suspicious side-eye during my AI lecture when Kevin made some coded comment about "erasing data."

I guess I learned two things today: always ask before you turn something into a cleaning implement, and Caltech upperclassmen have an incredible memory for embarrassing freshman moments. I just hope this incident doesn't become part of *my* Ditch Day lore for years to come. At least the common room looks spotless. And that shirt… well, it looks a lot cleaner now, too, even if it might be slightly less 'vintage' in appearance.

Time to drown my embarrassment in another cup of instant coffee and tackle this electromagnetism problem set.

-Trevor
