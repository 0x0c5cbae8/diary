Dear diary,

Sunday, August 16, 2026.

It’s only been two days since I officially moved into Bechtel, and my brain already feels like it's been hard-forked and recompiled. Caltech is… everything I imagined and more. The air literally crackles with intellect, or maybe that’s just the static electricity from all the brainpower rubbing together in the halls. I spent most of today just wandering around, getting gloriously lost between the Cahill Center and the Beckman Auditorium, then finding my way back to my dorm, which now feels like a familiar port in a storm of newness.

Orientation has been a whirlwind. So many names, so many faces, so many acronyms! I swear, they speak a whole other language here, and I’m just trying to download the dictionary. Everyone seems incredibly smart, which, okay, I *knew* going in, but it’s still humbling to be surrounded by people who casually discuss quantum entanglement over breakfast cereal. I even met my roommate, Alex. He's majoring in Planetary Science, which sounds ridiculously cool. We spent an hour trying to figure out the optimal path to the dining hall without hitting any "known freshman hazards" (aka, people trying to hand out flyers for clubs I haven't even heard of yet).

Flipping through the orientation packet tonight, past the bewildering map of the campus and the even more bewildering list of "optional" (but definitely not optional) workshops, I landed on the section about lab safety. And there it was: 'Required Lab Attire.' My mind immediately pictured the crisp white coat, the universal uniform of the aspiring scientist. I've always called them lab coats, but in my head, a silly thought popped up: my very own scientific *frock*. Sounds a bit archaic, doesn't it? Like something out of a Victorian science fiction novel, but there's a certain gravitas to it. A protective covering, a uniform that signifies a serious purpose.

I can’t wait to get mine. It feels like putting on a superhero costume, ready to dive into experiments, calculations, and maybe even blow something up (safely, of course). The thought of actually doing real research, of contributing to something meaningful, of wearing that "frock" with pride… it’s a little intimidating, but mostly exhilarating. Tomorrow, I think I’ll try to find the bookstore and see if they have a fitting guide. Or maybe I'll just ask Alex. He probably already knows the optimal frock-to-scientist ratio.

This is going to be an incredible adventure. My brain is tired, but my spirit is buzzing.

-Trevor
