Dear diary,

August 5th, 2026. Wednesday. Can you believe it? I'm actually *here*. Caltech. It feels surreal. My room in Lloyd House is… well, it’s a room. I spent most of the day trying to organize my life into two ridiculously small closets and a desk that looks like it's seen a thousand problem sets. Met some of the other freshmen wandering the halls, all looking equally bewildered and brilliant. One guy was already talking about quantum field theory, and I just nodded, trying to look like I knew *anything* about it beyond the Wikipedia summary I skimmed last year.

That's the overwhelming feeling right now – the sheer *potential*. I was sitting by Millikan Pond earlier, watching the ducks, and it just hit me. This isn't like high school, where the options felt pretty limited. Here, at Caltech, it feels like I could study *anything*. Really. I could dive into astrophysics and map black holes, or biomedical engineering and design the next generation of prosthetics. I could get involved in some crazy robotics project, or even contribute to a new theory of consciousness. The professors are literally working on the cutting edge of human knowledge, and they're right down the hall.

It’s exciting, terrifying, and a little bit paralyzing all at once. What if I pick the wrong thing? What if I’m not good enough for *anything* truly groundbreaking? Everyone here is so ridiculously smart. But then, another part of me thinks, "Trevor, this is what you wanted. This is the place where 'anything is possible' isn't just a motivational poster slogan, it's a daily reality." I guess the first step is just to show up, keep an open mind, and try *anything* that sparks even a tiny bit of curiosity. Even if it's just surviving Frosh Camp first. Wish me luck for orientation tomorrow.

-Trevor
