Dear diary,

Wednesday. What even *is* a Wednesday anymore? Just another blur between Tuesday's P-set due date and Thursday's lab prep. I swear my brain cells are undergoing some accelerated form of entropy. I spent six hours today debugging a single line of C++ for CS1 – a semicolon, Trevor, a single bloody semicolon! I almost started talking to my monitor. My roommate, Ben, just mumbled something about "the universe mocking my ambition" and went back to his own problem set. Probably right.

Then, dinner. Oh, dinner. Tonight's main course was "Mystery Meat Surprise," which looked suspiciously like last week's "Textured Protein Medley," just with a different, slightly more alarming shade of brown sauce. But that's not what got me. It was the lettuce. The salad bar lettuce. It was… aggressive. Each leaf was curled at the edges like it had just heard a really bad pun, and the color was less "vibrant green" and more "pre-apocalyptic swamp moss." I swear I saw a leaf try to make a break for it off my plate.

It actually made me think about Dr. Anya Sharma's intro bio lecture this morning on cellular turgor pressure. She was going on about how crucial water uptake is for maintaining the structural integrity of plant cells, using *Lactuca sativa* (that's lettuce, for anyone not fluent in scientific nomenclature yet) as her primary example. She even showed a time-lapse of a healthy lettuce leaf wilting dramatically. And here I am, witnessing the real-life, real-time advanced stages of *Lactuca sativa* senescence in the dining hall. It’s like Caltech finds a way to make everything, even my sad salad, a morbidly educational experience. I briefly considered taking a sample back to the lab to analyze its dehydration levels, but then decided I probably shouldn't bring biohazards into my dorm room, especially if I want to pass my next health and safety inspection. Besides, I'm pretty sure my TA would just give me a look that screams, "Trevor, go to sleep."

Maybe I need to start my own mini hydroponic garden in my room, just to experience a truly crisp leaf of romaine. Or maybe I should just stick to ramen for the rest of the quarter. At least I know what ramen is supposed to taste like and what it's *not* supposed to look like.

Anyway, gotta hit the books for physics. Gauss's Law isn't going to integrate itself.

-Trevor
