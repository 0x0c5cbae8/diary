Dear diary,

Ugh, Mondays. It’s only been a few weeks, but I swear Caltech Mondays are in a league of their own. Woke up feeling like my brain was still trying to parse the implications of last night’s existential crisis about the true nature of spacetime (thanks, roommate’s late-night musings), only to immediately dive into a day packed with lectures and problem sets. My coffee barely had a fighting chance.

Ph 1a was, as usual, a wild ride. Professor Chen moves at warp speed. Today we were deep into friction and forces, which seems simple enough on paper, right? Block on a ramp, fine. But then he introduced *the* problem: a block on an inclined plane, which itself was a *wedge* resting on a horizontal surface, with different coefficients of static and kinetic friction for each interface. My brain felt like it was trying to *wedge* a whole new coordinate system into place while simultaneously solving for static friction on two different interfaces, not to mention the normal forces changing depending on whether the block was sliding relative to the wedge, or the wedge relative to the table, or both!

For a good twenty minutes, I was completely lost. My pencil just hovered over the paper, unsure where to even begin drawing the free-body diagrams without creating a tangled mess of vectors. It’s insane how something so simple-looking can become so intricate here. They really love to *wedge* in all the complicating factors. But then, after a frantic re-read of the chapter and a quick whispered consultation with the guy next to me (who looked equally bewildered), something clicked. The trick was breaking it down, isolating the systems, and carefully considering the relative accelerations. When I finally started writing out the equations, one after another, and saw how they interlinked… it was a small victory, but a victory nonetheless. Even got the right sign convention for the friction! Take that, complicated wedge scenario.

Spent the rest of the afternoon in the library trying to make a *wedge* of time for my Ma 1c homework – those series are starting to get gnarly. Managed to snag a decent study spot, which felt like another win in the daily Caltech battle for resources. Now, to try and actually sleep before tomorrow’s onslaught. Wish me luck.

-Trevor
