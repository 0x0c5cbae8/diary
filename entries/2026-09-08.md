Dear diary,

Wow. Just… wow. It’s only September 8th, and my brain already feels like it’s been through a centrifuge. Caltech is everything they said it would be and more. The campus is beautiful, the people are brilliant (and a little intimidating, honestly), and I’m pretty sure I’ve already used more brainpower in the past 72 hours than I did all of senior year.

Today was particularly… *structural*. Our orientation group – the one I’m kind of tentatively calling my 'cohort' until we officially join Houses – had this engineering design challenge. The task? Build the tallest free-standing structure you can, using only dry spaghetti and a single bag of marshmallows as connectors. Sounds simple, right? Wrong. So, so wrong.

I thought, "Okay, physics! Triangulation, compression, tension." Easy peasy. We started with a wide base, trying to make a sturdy pyramid out of spaghetti sticks, using the marshmallows as squishy little joints. It promptly sagged. Then it collapsed. My initial vision of a soaring spaghetti skyscraper quickly devolved into a tangled mess, looking less like a feat of engineering and more like someone sneezed on a box of Bucatini.

Sarah, who seems to have a terrifyingly intuitive grasp of statics for a freshman, suggested we focus on smaller, interlocked squares rather than big triangles, and stack them. David, who’s already quoting obscure papers on material science, pointed out how the surface area of the marshmallows was crucial for distributing stress. Suddenly, my "common sense" approach was being deconstructed and rebuilt with actual *science*. We spent nearly two hours, hands sticky with marshmallow goo, breaking pasta, yelling (good-naturedly) at each other, and laughing when a carefully balanced section inevitably toppled.

By the end, our structure wasn't exactly the Eiffel Tower, but it stood. A wobbly, slightly asymmetrical tower of spaghetti and marshmallows, almost three feet tall. It felt like a monumental achievement. I mean, who knew pasta could be so… *educational*? It made me realize that even the simplest problems here require a whole new way of thinking. It was frustrating, exhilarating, and surprisingly fun, and I actually feel like I started to really *bond* with my group over our shared pasta-induced failures and eventual, shaky success.

After that intellectual workout, I headed to the dining hall for dinner. Guess what was on the menu? Penne with marinara. After spending hours meticulously analyzing the structural integrity of *dry* pasta, I found myself critically evaluating the *cooked* version. Is it al dente enough? Could this noodle withstand a tensile force of 5 Newtons before snapping? I swear, Caltech is already changing how I perceive everyday objects. I probably looked like a madman poking at my dinner with a fork.

It’s going to be a wild ride, diary. But if I can build a semi-stable tower out of spaghetti and make a few friends doing it, maybe I *can* survive my freshman year here.

-Trevor
