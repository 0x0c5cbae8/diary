Dear diary,

Wow. Just… wow. It's Saturday, and I thought I'd finally have a chance to properly explore campus, but no, Dr. Chen's 'Intro to Neurobiology' immersion experience isn't letting up, even on weekends. Not that I'm complaining, exactly. It's just… intense.

Today was all about vision. And by 'all about vision,' I mean *literally* all about the mechanics of how we see. We spent four hours in the lab, meticulously dissecting cow eyeballs. Cow! Eyeballs! My first reaction was a mix of 'Ew, gross' and 'This is the coolest thing I've ever done.' The sheer complexity of it, even in a cow, is mind-boggling. We had to carefully separate the sclera, identify the cornea, the iris, the lens… I swear, trying to get that vitreous humor out without rupturing anything felt like defusing a tiny, gelatinous bomb.

Dr. Chen was explaining how the retina works, how light hits the photoreceptors – rods and cones – and how those signals get sent down the optic nerve. It’s one thing to read about it in a textbook, but to hold an actual, albeit deceased, *eyeball* in your hand, to feel the firmness of the lens, to see the intricate layers… it just hits different. It's not just a diagram anymore; it's a real, biological marvel.

It made me think about all the times I've just 'eyeballed' a distance or 'kept an eyeball' on the time during a lecture. We use our eyes constantly, take them for granted. Now, after seeing the mechanics, I have a whole new appreciation. My own eyeballs feel a little more precious, a little more incredible, knowing what's going on inside them right now as I write this.

I'm exhausted, and I probably smell faintly of formaldehyde, but I also feel this incredible rush. This is Caltech, right? Getting your hands dirty (literally), grappling with complex systems, and having your mind blown by the universe, even if it's just the universe contained within a cow's eye.

Okay, time to try and wash the smell of science off me before dinner. Maybe I'll actually get to see the stars through the telescope tonight – *my* eyeballs, looking at the cosmos, powered by all these amazing cells.

-Trevor
