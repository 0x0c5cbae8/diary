Dear diary,

Another Sunday bites the dust, and with it, most of my remaining brain cells from a grueling week. Just finished up the last problem on the P-chem set – honestly, I'm not sure if the benzene ring or my brain cells are more stable at this point. Time to collapse, maybe rewatch a lecture or two before bed, you know, the usual Caltech "relaxing."

The weather today was actually decent, for Pasadena anyway. Managed to drag myself out for a quick run around campus, which was surprisingly refreshing. It’s hard to remember sometimes, amidst the never-ending stream of assignments and the distinct smell of burnt coffee in the common room, that there’s an actual world outside my dorm room and the library.

But the big win of the day, besides finally understanding orbital hybridization (mostly), was actually something a bit less glamorous. I finally hammered through the last module of the Chemical Hygiene Plan and Hazardous Waste Management training. It felt like it took forever! All those quizzes on spill protocols and proper PPE, clicking through slide after slide of regulations. Seriously, it's like they want you to become a certified safety inspector before they let you near a beaker.

And then, after the final quiz, there it was: a little pop-up window congratulating me, and an email shortly after with the attached PDF. My **certificate** of completion. It's not a degree, obviously, or even a minor, but it feels significant. This little digital piece of paper is my ticket, my golden key, to actually getting hands-on in one of the labs next term. Dr. Anya Sharma's group is doing some fascinating work on novel catalyst development, and she mentioned they might have space for a freshman to shadow and help out, *if* I had all my safety certifications.

So, while it was tedious, and I probably spent more time trying to stay awake than actually processing the finer points of GHS labeling, that certificate represents a concrete step towards doing actual science, not just theory. It’s a promise of future experiments, of seeing hypotheses tested in the real world, of contributing to something bigger than myself. It feels less like another requirement crossed off a list and more like an initiation. One small, slightly boring, certificate, one giant leap for this budding scientist. Now, to email Dr. Sharma and subtly drop that I'm fully compliant...

Wish me luck for the week ahead, it's going to be a long one.

-Trevor
