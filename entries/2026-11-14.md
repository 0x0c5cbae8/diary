Dear diary,

Another Saturday. It feels less like a weekend and more like "p-set catch-up day," but honestly, I wouldn't have it any other way. Spent most of the morning in Millikan, wrestling with Griffiths on some truly gnarly E&M problems. I swear, sometimes I think my brain cells are spontaneously combusting.

But the afternoon was good. A few of us from Blacker House (yeah, I’m still surprised I landed here, it’s… an experience!) decided to take a break. We’d been slaving over the initial proposal for our UROP project – trying to model some complex fluid dynamics using machine learning – and our brains were pretty fried. We decided to hit up Old Town Pasadena for some actual food that wasn’t from the dining hall.

As we were walking back, the late afternoon sun was hitting the dome of the Athenaeum just right, making it glow. And then, out of nowhere, this massive *flock* of starlings just erupted from the trees near Olive Walk. They swirled and dove, a living, breathing cloud of motion, completely synchronized. It was incredible, almost like they were one giant organism.

Watching them, I had this weird moment of clarity. Coming to Caltech, it felt like being thrown into the deep end, all alone. Everyone here is brilliant, and the pace is relentless. For a while, I honestly wondered if I'd ever find my footing, my *people*. But looking at that flock of birds, all moving together, it hit me. We’re all kind of like that here, aren't we? A bunch of incredibly smart, slightly-too-caffeinated individuals, but when we get together – whether it's battling a p-set, brainstorming a crazy project idea, or just complaining about how much sleep we *didn't* get – we become something more. A collective, a community. My *flock*.

It’s a strange comfort, knowing that even amidst all the insane challenges, I’m not really alone. And maybe, just maybe, being part of this particular flock of brilliant weirdos is exactly where I'm meant to be.

Now, back to the fluid dynamics. The real flocking behavior happens when we try to debug that code.

-Trevor
