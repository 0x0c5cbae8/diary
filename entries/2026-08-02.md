Dear diary,

Another Sunday here in Pasadena, and I’m already feeling the peculiar rhythm of Caltech life, even before classes properly begin. It’s August 2nd, the sun is blazing outside, and I’m mostly holed up in my room in Avery, enjoying the air conditioning and trying to process everything from orientation week. It’s been a whirlwind of meeting people, navigating campus, and learning more acronyms than I thought humanly possible.

Today was a bit quieter, which was nice. I spent the morning doing some laundry – a surprisingly complicated *campaign* against my own mountain of clothes – and then tried to decipher the labyrinthine logic of the library's catalog system. Seriously, I think it requires a specialized degree just to find a book sometimes.

But the real "campaign" I've been observing is all the election stuff. Posters are cropping up everywhere, not just for various clubs and interest groups trying to recruit us frosh, but for actual house positions. Treasurer, social chair, academic rep… it's a lot. I saw a few upperclassmen talking animatedly in the lounge earlier, clearly strategizing for someone's bid for Frosh Rep. It’s fascinating, honestly. Back in high school, elections were always a popularity contest, lots of empty promises and flashy slogans. Here, it seems… different. More about *utility*, I guess? People talking about specific initiatives, budgeting for house events, organizing study groups. It’s less about who can make the best speech and more about who can actually get things done.

I caught a snippet of someone saying, "We need a strong campaign this year if we're going to get the budget approved for the new coffee machine in the lounge." A *campaign* for a coffee machine! Only at Caltech. It makes me wonder what kind of campaigns I might get involved in. Could I ever see myself running for a house position? Or campaigning for a particular UROP project to get funding? It feels a bit daunting, like another layer of problem-solving I’m not quite equipped for yet. But also, a little exciting. To be able to advocate for something, to put in the effort, to *campaign* for a cause, big or small, within this community. It’s a very different kind of ambition than just getting good grades.

For now, though, my main campaign is just to remember everyone's name and not get lost on the way to the dining hall. Baby steps.

-Trevor
