Dear diary,

Another Sunday night, another existential dread about the week barreling down on me. It’s July 19th, 2026, and I swear the summer goes by faster at Caltech than anywhere else. My SURF project on quantum entanglement in photonic circuits is fascinating, truly, but it’s also a black hole for my time and energy. Plus, Dr. Albright *did* mention she’d like the preliminary data analysis by Friday, which is… aggressive, to say the least.

This weekend was supposed to be about "recharging." I slept in until 9 AM (a luxury!), did laundry, and tried to tackle the pile of papers threatening to engulf my desk. I even managed to squeeze in a quick run down Arroyo Seco – needed to clear my head. But then it was back to the lab, tweaking parameters for hours. My brain feels like scrambled eggs, but my room still looks like a disaster zone. My fridge contains only a questionable container of leftover pad thai and a half-empty carton of oat milk.

And that’s where the idea hit me, in a moment of pure, unadulterated exhaustion. What I wouldn't give for a… a manservant.

I know, I know, it sounds ridiculous. Straight out of a Victorian novel or some BBC period drama my mom used to watch. But honestly, imagine! Someone who just *knew* what needed doing. No PSETs for him, just perfectly folded laundry, a fully stocked fridge (with *actual food*), and my lab notebook meticulously organized and cross-referenced. He'd bring me a perfectly brewed cup of coffee (black, no sugar, precise temperature, obviously) right to my desk at 7 AM sharp, quietly remind me about that obscure departmental seminar, and maybe even iron my one decent shirt so I don't look like I wrestled a badger before my presentation.

He wouldn't need to understand quantum mechanics – just the mechanics of keeping my life from devolving into total chaos. My own personal Jeeves, but less sarcastic and more focused on ensuring my protein intake is adequate and my sleep cycle isn't completely inverted. He could field emails, schedule my appointments with Dr. Albright, and probably even debug some of the simpler Python scripts I've botched. Imagine the efficiency gains! My academic output would probably triple. I could focus 100% on the actual science, on building the future, instead of wondering if I have enough clean socks for Tuesday.

It's a childish fantasy, I know. I’m a Caltech freshman, not a lord of a sprawling estate. And honestly, the ethical implications of having someone else do all my mundane tasks in this day and age are probably… problematic. But in this moment, looking at my overflowing laundry hamper and the empty coffee mug graveyard on my desk, it feels like the most logical solution to optimizing my personal productivity. Maybe I should just build a robot for it. Now *that* sounds like a legitimate SURF project.

Okay, deep breaths. Time to prep for Dr. Albright's meeting and try to get a decent night's sleep. Another week, another set of challenges. At least I have my vivid imagination.

-Trevor
