Dear diary,

Another Tuesday, but this one felt… different. Woke up at 6 AM again, not by choice, but because my alarm clock app *voted* to go off despite my desperate pleas for five more minutes. Seriously, who designed a snooze button that shrinks when you're half-asleep?

Anyway, today was Election Day. My first one! It felt pretty surreal. I'd been planning my absentee ballot for weeks, making sure I understood the propositions, the candidates… The sheer amount of information was overwhelming, honestly. It's not like picking which flavor of Soylent to have for breakfast; this felt like a critical path analysis for the future of the republic. I mailed it last week, but still, seeing all the news coverage, the "Go Vote!" reminders plastered everywhere on campus… it made me actually stop and think for a minute between problem sets.

It’s weird how some choices feel so monumental, like who to *vote* for President, and others feel equally critical in a different way, like which derivation method to use for quantum mechanics, or which specific resistor value to pick for our circuit design. Earlier today, during our house meeting (which I almost slept through, oops), we actually had to *vote* on the theme for our upcoming Interhouse event. It was a heated debate between "Galactic Gala" and "Roaring Twenties Redux." I voted for Galactic Gala, obviously. Space is just objectively cooler than flappers, fight me. But then I realized, even in that small, trivial-seeming decision, there was a whole mini-democracy at play. Lobbying, arguments, compromises… it was like a microcosm of the actual election, just with slightly different stakes (like bragging rights over other houses).

I spent most of the afternoon holed up in Millikan, trying to finish my Physics 1 problem set. Dr. Chen always says to show our work, but sometimes I just want to *vote* for the answer to magically appear. Tonight I'll probably be refreshing FiveThirtyEight like a madman, even though I know I should be sleeping. It's strange, participating in something so big, even if it's just one tiny ballot in a sea of millions. Makes you feel… connected, I guess. Now, if only I could *vote* for more hours in the day.

-Trevor
