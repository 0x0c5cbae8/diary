Dear diary,

Happy Halloween, I guess? Though honestly, the scariest thing today was the prospect of having to choose between sleep and food this morning. Caltech truly redefines the concept of "weekend rest." But I digress.

Today was… intense. And exhilarating. The entire common room in Lloyd House has been a buzzing hive of activity (and a suspicious smell of old candy corn) since I dragged myself out of bed. Everyone’s been gearing up for the annual Halloween Hack. Or, rather, *hacks*. Plural, and therein lay the problem.

For the past few days, two major factions had emerged, each championing their own glorious vision for our House’s contribution to campus festivities. On one side, the 'Millikan Mystifiers,' led by David, a senior CS major whose code runs on pure caffeine and ambition, wanted to do a massive, complex projection mapping onto the façade of Millikan Library. Think 3D ghosts flying out of equations, or a giant monster devouring the dome. Technologically brilliant, incredibly Caltech, but also a logistical nightmare to pull off in under 24 hours, especially with the weather looking iffy.

On the other side, the 'Haunted Labyrinth Crew,' spearheaded by Maya, a sophomore MechE with boundless energy, proposed transforming the ground floor of our House into an elaborate, multi-room haunted maze. Lots of physical props, clever contraptions, and freshmen (like me, probably) dressed as jump-scare fodder. More hands-on, more interactive, but perhaps less of that iconic Caltech "wow, how did they *do* that?!" factor.

The tension peaked this afternoon. David was arguing the "prestige" and "algorithmic elegance" of the projection. Maya countered with "community engagement" and "experiential immersion." Both had valid points, detailed schematics, and armies of equally passionate supporters. The meeting was turning into a technical debate on resource allocation, person-hours, and risk assessment – essentially, a mini-conference on hack-onomics. No one was budging. We only had one budget, one pool of available hands, and a rapidly shrinking timeline.

That’s when I, the humble freshman, found myself speaking up. I'd been listening, trying to absorb the sheer brainpower flying around. I saw the genius in David's projection concept, and the fun in Maya's maze. "What if," I started, my voice a little reedy amidst the clamor, "we don't have to choose? What if we *negotiate* a hybrid? Instead of projecting onto Millikan, we could use a few of the projectors we already have, maybe those smaller ones from the auditorium, *inside* Maya's haunted maze. We could project dynamic, moving illusions – ghostly figures, morphing walls, optical trickery – directly onto the maze's surfaces. It leverages David's projection skills, but scales it to fit Maya's interactive experience. It's still technically challenging, but maybe more achievable in the timeframe, and it enhances the scares."

There was a moment of silence. I thought I’d made a fool of myself. Then, David slowly nodded. "Hm. Modular projection within a confined space... less ambient light interference, potentially higher resolution for the immediate effect..." Maya’s eyes lit up. "And we could have different themes for each room, with projected surprises! The physics lab could have projected quantum foam monsters!"

Suddenly, the negotiation wasn't a zero-sum game anymore. It was about synthesis. It took another hour to iron out the details – who was responsible for what, which projectors to use, how to synchronize the sound effects with the projections – but a consensus, a *solution*, emerged. The "Spectral Labyrinth of Lloyd House" was born.

It felt incredible to contribute, even in a small way, to such a high-stakes, high-intensity Caltech discussion. My brain is fried, and I still have a P-set due Monday, but seeing everyone now working together, building and coding and laughing, is just… awesome. I even found myself volunteering to be one of the projected quantum foam monsters. Maybe I *am* getting the hang of this place.

Now, to find a flashlight and some glow-in-the-dark paint.

-Trevor
