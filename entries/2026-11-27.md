Dear diary,

Friday. Post-Thanksgiving. More like Post-Apocalyptic P-set season, if you ask me. I swear, the only thing that actually took a break this week was my serotonin levels. Got back to campus this morning, the train was predictably packed, and I swear I could feel the collective dread radiating off every other student with a backpack full of textbooks.

Spent the whole afternoon wrestling with this Quantum Mechanics P-set. My brain feels like a particularly dense black hole – nothing escapes, and light just bends around it in confusion. And just when I thought I might finally, *finally*, grok the Pauli Exclusion Principle enough to write something coherent, the campus grapevine decided to go into overdrive.

So, apparently, there’s an *allegation* floating around, thick and heavy like the smog over Pasadena after a fire. It started as whispers in the dining hall, then morphed into slightly panicked texts. The gist is, a TA in the Intro to Chem lab section – not mine, thankfully, but one that shares a lot of people with my Math 1b class – apparently found some... *striking similarities* between a submitted lab report from last week and an older solution set he found online. Not even a completely different problem, but practically a word-for-word section for the synthesis part.

My stomach dropped when I heard it. Caltech doesn't mess around with academic integrity. You hear the stories during frosh orientation, the dire warnings about the Honor Code. But it always feels so abstract, something that happens to 'other people.' Now, it feels like it’s happening right next door. Or maybe to someone I’ve actually had coffee with.

It just makes me paranoid, you know? I spent an extra hour going back over my own Thermodynamics P-set, double-checking every single reference, making sure my wording was unmistakably my own, even for things that feel like common knowledge. Did I accidentally phrase something too similarly to a textbook? Is my work truly original? It’s enough to make you second-guess every single sentence you write, even if you know you didn't cheat.

I guess it's a harsh reminder that even though we're all in this insane academic pressure cooker together, there are lines you just can't cross. And someone might have. Hope whoever it is gets through it okay, but also, hope the system works. My head hurts. Back to quantum weirdness.

-Trevor
