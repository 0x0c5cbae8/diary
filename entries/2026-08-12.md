Dear diary,

August 12th, 2026. Wednesday. Can't believe it's already Wednesday. Feels like I've been here a lifetime and five minutes all at once. My brain is officially a bowl of scrambled eggs, but in a good way, mostly.

The orientation schedule is absolutely relentless. Today was the 'Intro to Computational Tools' workshop, and man, they threw a lot at us. We breezed through a bit of Python and MATLAB, which I kinda knew from high school, but then Professor Davies, who seems to genuinely *love* all things numeric, started talking about this program called *Maple*.

At first, it just looked like another one of those dense, academic-looking interfaces with a million buttons I'll never understand. Then she dropped a truly gnarly multi-variable integral on us – something that would have taken me pages and hours back home – and told us to try and solve it symbolically using *Maple*.

I swear, my brain felt like it was trying to chew through concrete. All the syntax, the specific commands for `Int` and `value()`, getting the variables just right… I kept messing up parentheses and getting error messages that looked like ancient hieroglyphs. But then, after almost an hour of furious typing, deleting, and a lot of confused glances at my peers who looked equally stumped, I finally got it! Seeing that incredibly complex expression simplify down to something elegant and concise, all neatly formatted, felt like I'd just scaled Everest in flip-flops.

It's moments like these that make me realize how different Caltech is going to be. Everything feels like a new puzzle, and the tools they give you are incredibly powerful but require so much learning. My brain is definitely going to be *stretched* this year. This *Maple* software feels like a metaphor for Caltech itself – intimidating at first, but incredibly powerful once you figure out how to wield it.

I even managed to sneak in a quick search for "maple syrup" on my break just to give my brain something comforting after all that symbolic manipulation. Didn't help the integral, but it did make me hungry for breakfast.

Anyway, tomorrow is the ethics in science seminar. Hopefully, less syntax, more philosophy. Wish me luck!

-Trevor
