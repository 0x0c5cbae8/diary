Dear diary,

Another Thursday blurs into another Friday eve. Is it even Thursday? My internal clock is currently operating on "whenever Dr. Chen needs this data processed" time. Been holed up in the lab all day, trying to get this Python script to run without spitting out a gazillion error messages. My SURF project on cellular automata for protein folding is fascinating, truly, but debugging feels like wrestling a particularly stubborn, syntax-error-generating octopus.

Today was all about the "copy." Not in the "let me *copy* your PSET" kind of way – thankfully, those days are mostly over, replaced by the joy of *copying* complex literature into my brain. No, today it was about *copying* a function. Dr. Chen, my grad student mentor, gave me this snippet of code from his previous work and basically said, "Implement this, but make sure it can handle variable input dimensions." Simple enough, right? Wrong. The original function was elegant, but trying to adapt it without just doing a literal cut-and-paste felt like trying to write a sonnet in a language I'd only just started learning.

I spent hours poring over the lines, trying to understand the logic behind each step, rather than just mechanically *copying* and tweaking. It’s so tempting, when you're tired and the deadline looms, to just *copy* an existing solution from Stack Overflow, or even Dr. Chen's script itself, and hope it works. But that's not how you learn. That's not how you become a scientist, capable of creating something truly novel. You have to internalize it, break it down, understand why *this* line does *that*. It’s a Caltech mantra, I guess, not to just take things at face value.

It was only after like, my fifth coffee and a short walk around the SAC (just to clear my head, not for a workout, obviously) that it clicked. The key wasn't in *copying* the exact structure, but in *copying* the underlying mathematical principle it implemented. Once I understood that, the actual code became an expression of that principle, not just a *copy* of someone else's solution. And lo and behold, a few more hours of frustrated typing later, it finally ran. No errors. Just pure, glorious output.

Now to *copy* this success to the next module. Wish me luck. I think I can finally *copy* myself to bed.

-Trevor
