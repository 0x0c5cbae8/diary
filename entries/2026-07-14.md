Dear diary,

Another Tuesday, another gallon of lukewarm coffee. The lab air conditioning is fighting a losing battle against the Pasadena summer, which means I'm pretty sure my brain is slowly melting out my ears. Spent most of the morning wrestling with the SEM again, trying to get those high-res images of our scaffolds. The resolution is just *not* cooperating, and I’m pretty sure the machine knows it.

My SURF project, as you know, is all about biomimetic materials – specifically, figuring out the precise conditions for *in vitro* apatite formation on various polymer matrices. Essentially, trying to coax calcium and phosphate ions into depositing themselves perfectly to form bone-like structures. It’s mind-boggling how the human body does this so elegantly and routinely. And when it doesn’t, or when it happens in the wrong place, that’s when you get all sorts of problems – unwanted *calcification*. Atherosclerosis, kidney stones, stiff joints. It’s like the body’s own internal concrete mixer going rogue.

Thinking about that today, about how *calcification* can go from a vital process to a pathological one, got me thinking about other things that can get "hardened" when they shouldn't. Like ideas. Or even people.

I was chatting with Dr. Chen over lunch – well, mostly he was lamenting, and I was nodding sagely while trying not to spill my ramen. He was talking about the difficulties in securing funding for his more "out there" theories, new approaches to protein folding that challenge some long-held assumptions. He sighed and said something like, "Sometimes, Trevor, the academic establishment just gets too *calcified* in its thinking. It resists anything that doesn't fit neatly into the existing dogma, even when the evidence is staring them in the face."

And I totally get what he means. Here at Caltech, we're taught to be rigorous, to follow protocols, to replicate results, to build carefully on existing knowledge. Which is crucial, obviously. But sometimes, I worry that the sheer volume of information, the constant grind of problem sets, the pressure to *know* the "right" answers, could make my own thoughts start to *calcify* if I’m not careful. It’s easy to get rigid, to stop questioning the fundamentals, to just absorb information without critically dissecting it. To forget the thrill of the "what if?"

It's a fine line between building a solid, foundational understanding and letting your intellectual curiosity become rigid and brittle. I need to remember to keep exploring, keep questioning, keep pushing beyond the accepted narratives. To not let my brain turn into one of those unwanted apatite deposits. And maybe, just maybe, drink some actual water instead of just coffee. Hydration prevents *calcification*, right? At least biologically speaking.

-Trevor
