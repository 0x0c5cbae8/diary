Dear diary,

Saturday. Already. This week felt like a compressed semester. Core classes are no joke – it’s like trying to drink from a firehose, only the firehose is spewing differential equations and quantum mechanics. My brain feels perpetually a few clock cycles behind. Seriously, how do people here make it look so easy? I spent most of Friday night trying to debug a simple Python script for CS 1, and I think I actually saw the sun rise. Whoops.

So, today was supposed to be a "recharge" day. I told myself no more problem sets, no more coding. Just... relax. Naturally, that lasted about an hour. My brain apparently isn't wired for idleness. Instead, I decided to tackle a micro-project I’d been vaguely planning since move-in: building a smart light for my desk that changes color based on the local weather forecast. Totally unnecessary, totally fun.

Problem was, I needed a specific type of ESP32 breakout board and a pack of addressable LEDs that I hadn't managed to snag before leaving home. After a quick search on my phone, I found an address for "Pasadena Electronics Surplus" about a mile and a half away. Figured a walk would be good.

The place... wow. It was less of a store and more of an archaeological dig site for circuit boards. Rows of dusty shelves stacked to the ceiling with every conceivable capacitor, resistor, wire, and widget you could imagine. Boxes labeled with faded Sharpie: "Vintage Vacuum Tubes," "Assorted Relays," "Mismatched Power Supplies." It felt less like a retail establishment and more like a *depot*. A massive, unorganized **depot** of forgotten tech, discarded prototypes, and components waiting for their next big idea.

I spent almost an hour just wandering the aisles, smelling that distinct old-electronics smell. It was mesmerizing. I found my ESP32 board wedged between a pile of old VCR parts and some surprisingly robust looking industrial sensors. Even got a discount because the owner (a gruff but kindly man named Stan who looked like he’d been soldering since the invention of electricity) said it had been sitting there "since before Wi-Fi was a thing."

Walking back, with my little bag of electronic treasure, I felt a strange sense of belonging. This whole place – Caltech, Pasadena, even that dusty old electronics *depot* – it all feels like a giant playground for the curious. It’s a little intimidating, sure, but also incredibly exciting. My "recharge" day didn’t involve much relaxation, but it definitely recharged something else: my inner tinkerer. And now, to get this weather light working!

-Trevor
