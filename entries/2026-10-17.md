Dear diary,

Saturday, October 17, 2026

Another Saturday, another mountain of p-sets. Woke up late-ish, around 9 AM, which felt like a luxury after pulling an all-nighter for that CS 1 midterm earlier this week. My brain still feels a bit like overcooked spaghetti. Anyway, I spent the morning trying to conquer Phys 1a p-set 5. Specifically, that *dreaded* problem about the coupled oscillators in a non-inertial frame. My head was spinning faster than the fictitious forces I was trying to account for.

I stared at it for an hour, then two, re-reading the textbook, watching a couple of Feynman lectures that mostly made me feel even dumber. I was about to give up and just accept the inevitable lost points when I remembered seeing an email from the physics department about a peer-led **workshop** this afternoon. Usually, I try to muscle through things on my own – a bit of stubborn pride, I guess – but today, pride was definitely losing to desperation.

So, I dragged myself over to East Bridge around 2 PM. The room was already packed with other frosh, all looking equally bewildered and slightly sleep-deprived. An upperclassman, a junior named Maya from Fleming, was leading it. She’s famous for being super smart but also really good at explaining things without making you feel like an idiot. And thank goodness for that.

She started by breaking down the core concepts for the oscillating system, sketching out various forces, and then, *finally*, she got to the trickiest part: transforming to the rotating frame. She didn't just give us the answer, of course, but she walked us through the thought process, step-by-step, patiently answering every "Why is *that* a Coriolis force?" and "Where did that term come from?" question. It was like watching someone untangle a ridiculously complex knot, and suddenly, the whole mess started to make sense. My pen was flying, copying down her diagrams and explanations.

By the time the two-hour workshop was over, I felt a significant weight lift off my shoulders. I didn't *completely* solve the problem in the room, but I left with a clear path forward and, more importantly, a much better understanding of the underlying physics. It's crazy how much difference it makes to have someone break down the jargon and just explain it in plain English, with a bit of humor thrown in.

Now, back in my room, fueled by a cup of strong coffee from Chandler, I'm actually making progress on that p-set. Still a lot to do before Monday, but at least the biggest monster on my plate just got a little less scary. These workshops are a lifesaver, honestly. Probably wouldn't survive frosh year without them.

-Trevor
