Dear diary,

Well, day two (or maybe three? Time is definitely a construct the moment you stop having parents tell you to wake up for school) in Bechtel, and I think I've successfully navigated the laundry room without shrinking anything important. Small victories, right? The welcome packet for frosh week is thicker than some textbooks I've seen, and I've already signed up for four different introductory talks, two club fairs, and a "Pasadena Scavenger Hunt" that sounds suspiciously like a test of my ability to decipher obscure historical markers. My roommate, Liam, seems chill. He's already got his entire setup color-coded, which makes my pile of unopened boxes look like a disaster zone.

I just hung up from a video call with Mom. It was good to hear a familiar voice, even if half the conversation was her asking if I've eaten anything other than ramen (the answer is yes, sometimes there's pizza). Mostly, though, she was buzzing about my older sister, Sarah. Apparently, Sarah and Kevin (her fiancé) finally nailed down a wedding date for next spring, and Mom just had a marathon planning session with Kevin's mother.

And that's where the "mother-in-law" thought really kicked in. Mom was describing the various... *suggestions* that Kevin's mom had regarding everything from the floral arrangements to the seating chart. She wasn't complaining, not really, but there was a certain tone in her voice, a specific set of eye rolls, that I've learned to interpret over the years. It made me realize that even if I eventually manage to master quantum mechanics or build a functional fusion reactor, understanding the intricate, often baffling, dynamics of in-law relationships might be an even greater challenge.

I mean, from a purely anthropological perspective, it's fascinating. You have two distinct family units, each with their own established customs, inside jokes, and deeply ingrained habits, suddenly attempting a full-merge operation. And at the nexus, often, is the *mother-in-law*. It's like a complex systems engineering problem with highly emotional, unpredictable variables. Is there a universal algorithm for successful inter-familial integration? Does the 'mother-in-law' stereotype stem from some evolutionary imperative to protect the gene pool, or is it purely a social construct, like the arbitrary designation of 'Thursday'? I briefly considered sketching out a flowchart for Sarah and Kevin to navigate their various familial obligations, complete with decision nodes and weighted probabilities for emotional fallout, but I suspect that might not be received as well as my current, less analytical approach of "offer to help carry boxes."

Anyway, it's strange. Here I am, embarking on this entirely new chapter of my life, surrounded by strangers and equations, and my brain is still back home, trying to decipher the complex social algorithms of impending matrimony. Maybe this is good training for Caltech after all – learning to dissect complex systems, even when they involve a lot of passive-aggressive commentary about the choice of canapés.

Tomorrow, more orientation. I'm hoping it involves fewer discussions of floral arrangements and more about, you know, actual science.

-Trevor
