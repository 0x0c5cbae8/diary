Dear diary,

Okay, it’s Thursday evening, and I’m pretty sure my brain just hit its weekly maximum capacity. Ma 1a, Ph 1a, and Ch 1a P-sets are all circling like hungry vultures, and I swear Dr. Zaremba’s lecture today was 90% Greek letters and 10% existential dread. I felt like my brain was trying to debug a quantum entanglement problem while simultaneously solving for the gravitational constant of a black hole made entirely of caffeine.

After staring at a particularly stubborn differential equation for about an hour without making any progress (my desk lamp was mocking me, I swear), I decided I needed to get out. Not just out of my room, but *up*. So, I grabbed my hoodie, my trusty old binoculars (you never know what you might see, right?), and made my way to the emergency stairwell that leads to the north section of the Fleming House **roof**.

It’s not exactly officially sanctioned for casual hangs, but everyone does it. The cool night air hit me, and for the first time all day, I could actually breathe. Pasadena isn't exactly a dark sky preserve, but from up here, away from the immediate glare, the stars felt a little closer. I found Orion's Belt pretty quickly – it's always reassuringly there.

But it wasn't just the stars I was looking at. From this vantage point, I could see across campus, a patchwork of red tile and concrete. I spent a good twenty minutes just scoping out the other buildings, imagining all the famous Caltech "hacks" that have unfolded over the decades. I mean, putting the Rose Bowl scoreboard on Millikan Library's **roof**? Or the time they rearranged shrubs to spell out "Caltech" visible from space? It's legendary stuff. You can almost feel the mischievous energy still lingering.

I even zoomed in with my binoculars on a couple of distant **roofs**, wondering if anyone was up there right now, plotting something equally audacious. Or maybe just another stressed freshman like me, looking for a moment of peace. It’s funny, the same structures that shelter us from the rain and sun also provide this incredible perspective, this literal high ground for contemplation (and potentially, future shenanigans).

The P-sets are still waiting, and the Greek letters haven't magically transformed into English, but being up there, feeling the slight breeze and seeing the campus lights twinkling below, just gave me a different kind of energy. It’s like a reminder that even when things feel overwhelming, there’s always a new angle to view the problem from, maybe even from a **roof**. And maybe, just maybe, I’ll be part of some future legend involving one of these **roofs** myself. One day. After I finish this P-set.

-Trevor
