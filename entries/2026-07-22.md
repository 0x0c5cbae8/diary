Dear diary,

Another Wednesday, another late night in the lab. My brain feels like it’s been run through a particle accelerator – slightly fragmented, definitely high-energy. This SURF in experimental particle physics is fascinating, but also just… *a lot*. Dr. Chen keeps throwing these datasets at us, expecting us to just intuitively "see" the patterns, which, spoiler alert, is not how my intuition works at 11:30 PM after 14 hours of staring at collision events.

Today, though, the word of the day was "flavor." Not the kind you get from the dreadful Dining Hall's attempt at "international cuisine," which, let's be honest, often tastes like existential dread with a side of questionable protein. No, this "flavor" refers to quarks. Up, Down, Strange, Charm, Top, Bottom. Six of them, each a distinct type, or 'flavor.' It’s such a bizarre, humanistic term for something so fundamental and abstract. Who decided to call them 'flavors'? Did they imagine a little cosmic chef mixing ingredients for the universe? Makes me wonder what the "flavor" of a dark matter particle would be. Probably something bland and elusive, like eating mist.

We were trying to identify decay products from a particular particle interaction, essentially trying to pick out the signatures of different quark "flavors" amidst all the noise. It’s like trying to discern the subtle notes of a gourmet dish when someone’s just dumped a whole spice rack into it. So much data, so many variables. I spent three hours debugging a Python script that was supposed to filter out background noise, only to realize I'd flipped a boolean. My advisor just chuckled and said, "Ah, the subtle flavor of a good bug." I wanted to groan.

But seriously, the more I delve into this, the more I appreciate the elegance, even the *flavor*, of these fundamental concepts. It's a different kind of taste – not sweet or sour, but intellectually satisfying. Even if it does make my brain hurt. I grabbed a bag of those really artificial-tasting cheesy crackers on the way back to Fleming. Contradictory, I know. My mind is wrestling with the universe's fundamental building blocks, while my mouth is happy with synthesized cheese powder. Maybe that's the "flavor" of being a Caltech freshman – a constant battle between high-minded science and very basic human needs.

Still have to finish that problem set for CS 10. Wish I could download the "flavor" of sleep right now.

-Trevor
