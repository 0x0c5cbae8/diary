Dear diary,

Another Monday devoured. My brain feels like a half-chewed P-set, all logic fragments and frayed nerves. Today was especially brutal. Between the Thermodynamics lecture that felt like a direct assault on my understanding of entropy, and then trying to debug that MIE (Measurement & Instrumentation Experience) code for three hours straight, I'm pretty sure I permanently lost a few neurons.

But the real kicker was the problem session for Quantum. We were going through the derivations for some perturbation theory stuff, and Professor Davies asked me to walk through a step. I *knew* the answer, I could see it in my head, but actually articulating it? It came out like a tangled mess of half-formed thoughts and "uhms." Afterward, my friend Ben, who always has a knack for cutting to the chase, just gave me a look that screamed, "Dude, clarity!"

It got me thinking about communication, really. Like, truly transferring an idea from one brain to another. It's not just about knowing the stuff; it's about being able to package it up neatly and send it off. Later, I was flipping through some old literature (don't ask why, probably a procrastination tactic) and came across the word "communicant." Instantly, my brain latched onto it.

My first thought, naturally, was the religious context – someone receiving communion. But then my Caltech brain took over, trying to generalize. What if "communicant" just meant someone *participating* in an act of communication? Someone who isn't just speaking or listening, but actively engaging in the exchange, both sending and receiving information, understanding the shared ritual of knowledge transfer.

In a way, aren't we all communicants here? Every time I try to explain a solution to Ben, or he tries to walk me through a tricky proof, we're acting as communicants. We're offering up pieces of understanding, hoping the other person can internalize them. And then we're receiving their feedback, their questions, their own offerings. It's a shared experience of trying to find common ground in a sea of complex concepts.

The precision needed in science, in math, is almost like a sacred language. One misplaced variable, one ambiguous sentence, and the entire "message" gets corrupted. I realize now that being a good scientist isn't just about solving problems, it's about being an excellent communicant of those solutions. It’s about being precise enough that your "offering" of knowledge is truly received and understood, without needing a priestly interpreter.

I need to work on being a better communicant in this Caltech cosmos. Maybe tomorrow I'll try explaining that Quantum step to Ben again, but this time, with the clarity of a carefully constructed sacrament. Or, you know, just a really well-organized proof.

-Trevor
