Dear diary,

August 27th, 2026. Thursday. Wow. Just… wow. Is it even possible for four days to feel like a lifetime and five minutes all at once? My brain feels like it’s been put through a cosmic particle accelerator. Caltech is a whole different beast than I imagined. Every conversation is like a mini-lecture, every meal a potential collaboration. I met a girl in the dining hall today who’s already outlining plans for a freshman project involving neural networks for identifying exoplanets. *Exoplanets.* And here I am, still trying to remember which direction my dorm room faces.

Today was packed with more orientation stuff – 'Navigating the Caltech Ecosystem,' a 'Welcome to Research' panel, and a deeply unsettling talk about time management where they basically implied sleep was a quaint, pre-Caltech concept. My head is buzzing with information, possibilities, and a healthy dose of existential dread about problem sets I haven't even seen yet. I'm excited, truly, but also feeling this weird pull, like I'm simultaneously soaring and about to crash.

It was during one of those moments, feeling utterly overwhelmed back in my room, that I dug out my old wooden **yoyo** from my unpacked box. I hadn't touched it in ages, but the familiar weight and smooth texture in my hand was instantly grounding. Just letting it drop, feeling the string unwind, then the satisfying tug as it spun back up. Down, up. Down, up. It's such a simple mechanism, yet my Caltech-wired brain immediately started breaking it down. The conservation of angular momentum as it 'sleeps' at the bottom, the conversion of kinetic to potential energy as it climbs, the gyroscopic stability keeping it spinning true. There's a deceptively elegant bit of physics wrapped up in this toy.

And honestly, my emotions feel a lot like that yoyo right now. One minute I'm high up, filled with adrenaline and excitement for all the groundbreaking things I'm going to learn and do here. The next, I'm plummeting down, wondering if I'm even remotely capable of keeping up with these absolute geniuses, fearing I'll get tangled in the string of expectations and just stop spinning. Will I 'sleep' through an important lecture? Will I run out of energy trying to master all these new skills?

But then it comes back up, right? The yoyo always returns to your hand, no matter how far it drops. It's a cycle. Maybe Caltech will be like that – a series of dizzying ups and downs, but ultimately, I'll learn how to master the spin, how to keep it going. For now, the rhythmic hum of the string is a good reminder to breathe. And maybe, just maybe, I’ll find a way to apply some differential equations to optimize my sleep duration. One can dream.

-Trevor
