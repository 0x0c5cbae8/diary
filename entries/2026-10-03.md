Dear diary,

October 3, 2026 (Saturday)

It’s Saturday, and for the first time in what feels like ages, I actually slept past 7 AM. Glorious. Though, even then, my brain was already spinning through the thermodynamics p-set due Monday and the initial readings for my Humanities course. The Caltech "weekend" is definitely more of a conceptual framework than a reality, but hey, at least I got coffee without a mad dash.

This morning was… interesting. I walked out of my room, still half-asleep, and noticed a bright orange paper taped to the door of Mark's room down the hall. Then another on Sarah's, and then… yep, one on mine. It was a mock "Eviction Notice," printed with official-looking (but clearly fake) Caltech seals. Mine read: "NOTICE OF EVICTION – Premises: Trevor's Desk, Room X. Grounds for Eviction: Excessive accumulation of unresolved problem sets creating a gravitational anomaly detrimental to the overall intellectual equilibrium of Dabney House. Immediate Remediation Required."

I actually laughed out loud. Apparently, this is a house tradition during "House Clean-Up Week," which nobody told me about. The upperclassmen go around on a Saturday morning, assess the level of chaos, and issue these hilarious, semi-scathing eviction notices. Mark's was for "failure to dispose of ancient pizza boxes, thereby fostering an unsanctioned fungal colony." Sarah's was for "establishing an unauthorized Dyson sphere of coffee cups around her monitor." It’s so absurd, but also, so perfectly Caltech. It’s a way to gently nudge people to tidy up without being preachy, and to bond over shared messiness and the ever-present p-set grind.

It got me thinking about the word "eviction" though. Here, it’s a joke, a fun, quirky part of the house culture. But for a freshman at Caltech, the idea of a *real* eviction – academic, that is – feels like it’s constantly lurking in the background. The sheer intensity of the coursework, the feeling of always being on the edge of comprehension, the constant pressure to perform… it’s a different kind of "notice." The one that whispers, "If you can't keep up, you might find yourself evicted from this place." It’s a heavy thought sometimes, especially when a p-set feels particularly insurmountable.

But then I look at my fake eviction notice, mocking my pile of unstarted (and finished) thermodynamics problems, and I realize it’s also a reminder that we’re all in this together. We're all fighting the good fight against gravitational anomalies of p-sets. And for now, my desk, and I, are safe. Time to tackle that thermo p-set before my eviction becomes permanent.

-Trevor
