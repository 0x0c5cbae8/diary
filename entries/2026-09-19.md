Dear diary,

Saturday. Who knew a weekend could feel so much like an extension of the week? I woke up at 7:30, which felt like sleeping in, honestly. Had a quick breakfast at Avery, then straight to the library. Quantum Mech problem set isn’t going to solve itself, and Professor Chen’s lectures move at light speed. It’s only the third week, and I already feel like my brain is running a marathon while simultaneously trying to understand string theory.

Spent about four hours trying to debug a single line of code for my CS 1 project. It was one of those infuriating bugs where the code *looked* perfectly fine, but just wouldn't compile. My roommate, Alex, popped in to grab his charger and just sighed dramatically when he saw my screen. "Still at it, huh?" he said. I just grunted. I could feel the frustration building, a tight knot behind my eyes. I swear, one more syntax error, and I was going to *snap*. Seriously, just completely lose my cool and maybe throw my laptop out the window. (Not really, it’s a leased laptop, and I can’t afford that kind of replacement fee).

Just as I was about to give up and grab another coffee, I leaned back in my chair, stretching my stiff neck. And that’s when it happened – a sharp, unmistakable *snap* from somewhere underneath me. My heart lurched. I froze, expecting the chair to collapse, or maybe I’d broken something vital in my back from hunching over all week. But nothing. I twisted around, peering under the seat. It was just the small plastic cap covering one of the chair’s wheel mechanisms. It had popped off and skittered across the floor.

It was such a minor thing, but that loud, sudden noise just… reset me. For a second, I almost laughed. All that built-up tension, the feeling of being on the verge of snapping myself, and it was just a cheap plastic cap. It reminded me to just breathe. That maybe I’m pushing myself a bit too hard already. Caltech is amazing, and terrifying, and exhilarating all at once. I don't want to burn out before midterms even start.

I found the cap, snapped it back into place, and then, for some reason, the bug just *clicked*. A missing semicolon. Of course. It was always a missing semicolon.

I think I'll go for a walk now. Get some actual fresh air. Maybe even *snap* a photo of the palm trees against the sunset. Need to remember there’s more to life here than PSETs and coding errors.

-Trevor
