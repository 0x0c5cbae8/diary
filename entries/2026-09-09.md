Dear diary,

Another Wednesday, another existential crisis brought to you by Ma 1a. P-set 1 is officially my nemesis. I swear I spent three hours staring at the same differential equation today, and it just stared back, mocking me with its elegant, unsolvable (to me) form. My brain feels like a wrung-out sponge, and the only thing I've successfully calculated today is how many hours of sleep I'm *not* going to get tonight. Seriously, how do people even *start* these things?

Mid-afternoon, I hit a wall. Just couldn't read another line of Griffiths' QM textbook for Ph 1a. My eyes were blurring. That's when I remembered seeing a flyer in Lloyd House for the Caltech Robotics Club's "Introduction to ROS" talk – "light refreshments provided." Honestly, the "refreshments" part was what really caught my eye. Not because I was starving, but because the *promise* of them felt like an official permission slip to *stop* thinking about integrals and quantum states and *start* thinking about something else, even for an hour. A mental palate cleanser, a sanctioned break from the relentless academic grind.

I trudged over to the Annenberg building, my backpack feeling like it was full of lead. The "light refreshments" turned out to be a cooler full of canned soda (mostly diet, of course) and a couple of bags of chips. Predictable, a true Caltech classic of 'barely-there' hospitality, but honestly, even a slightly-warm Mountain Dew felt like a luxury after a day of pure cognitive drain. I grabbed a can, leaned against the back wall, and tried to absorb what they were saying about Robot Operating System. Most of it flew over my head – way too much advanced coding for a freshman still wrestling with Python basics – but it was *different*. It was *forward-looking*. It was *not* Ma 1a.

And you know what? Sipping that slightly-too-sweet soda, and just being in a room with other people who were clearly excited about *something*, was actually incredibly... *refreshing*. Not just the drink itself, but the entire experience. It was a tangible break, a mental reset button. I even ended up chatting briefly with another freshman, Sarah, about how we both felt overwhelmed but also kind of thrilled to be here. We bonded over the surprisingly robust selection of chips (BBQ was a win). It was a moment of connection in the midst of the Caltech hurricane.

I'm back in my room now, the Mountain Dew buzz long gone, and the Ma 1a p-set is still glowering at me from my desk. But for that hour, I genuinely felt my brain *defrost* a little. It was a small, unassuming moment, powered by cheap sugar and salt, but it reminded me that there are other things happening here besides equations and late nights. Sometimes, all it takes is a bag of chips and a half-decent soda to remember that I'm more than just a p-set-solving machine. I'm Trevor, and I occasionally need refreshments for my soul.

-Trevor
