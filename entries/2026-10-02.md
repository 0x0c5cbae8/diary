Dear diary,

Another week down. It’s Friday, 2026-10-02, and honestly, I feel like my brain has been put through a high-speed centrifuge. The past few days have been a blur of quantum mechanics p-sets, a particularly brutal multivariable calc quiz that I’m pretty sure tried to break my spirit, and endless debugging. I’ve probably consumed enough coffee to power a small city block by now.

But the real struggle, the one that’s been haunting my waking hours and probably my subconscious too, is the ME 10 project. We’re tasked with building a small, articulated mechanism that can perform a simple task. Mine is supposed to be a little bipedal walker – nothing fancy, just a couple of servos, some laser-cut acrylic, and an Arduino. Sounds straightforward, right? *Right.*

Instead, what I have sitting on my desk in Dabney lounge, surrounded by discarded wires and a growing mountain of SPOP wrappers, is a disaster. It’s supposed to stand up and take a step, but every time I power it on, it just sort of… crumples. It has these skinny little legs and a boxy torso, and when it inevitably face-plants, all its joints just give way in a really pathetic, lifeless sort of way. I swear, it looks exactly like one of those old, jointed wooden artist’s *dolls* when it’s gone limp, just without the painted smile.

I spent six hours tonight, until 3 AM, just trying to calibrate the servo angles. It’s supposed to mimic a human gait, but mine looks like it’s having a seizure before collapsing into a pile of plastic kindling. Is the code wrong? Are the servos twitchy? Did I mess up the wiring diagram *again*? I keep staring at this inert, doll-like heap, willing it to move, but it just stares back with its blank, acrylic face. I feel like I'm trying to teach a particularly stubborn toy to dance, and it's just refusing.

At this rate, the only thing it’s going to be good for is being a desk ornament, perhaps a mascot for failure. I even briefly considered just giving up and buying one of those generic robot kits, but that's a total Vellany violation, and frankly, my pride wouldn't let me. Plus, the Professor has a way of looking at you that says, "I know you didn't build that from scratch."

Tomorrow I'm going to grab an early breakfast, hit up the Electronics Shop for some new servos, and then dive back into the code. Maybe a fresh pair of eyes and a full night’s sleep (a girl can dream) will help me figure out why my little plastic doll-bot refuses to obey its creator. Or maybe I’ll just build it a tiny wheelchair.

-Trevor
